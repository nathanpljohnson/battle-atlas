# Independent review: Austerlitz (v2 draft)

This review was written by an independent reviewer agent, following `tools/review_prompt.md`, on 8 Oct 2026. It is saved here in summary, keeping every finding. The author's responses are in `brief.md`, under "Review responses".

## Verdict
**Not yet fit to publish as drafted**, though close.
- **What is sound:** the French order of battle, the brigade names and the place identifications are mostly right, and the brief is unusually transparent about source conflicts.
- **What must change first:**
  - One unit is visibly mispositioned: `col1` stands in the Satschan pond at 15:30.
  - The brief mis-explains the West Point strength labels.
  - Two attributions are wrong: the 4e Ligne's eagle, and who fired on the ice.
  - Too much brigade-level detail rests on popular secondary sites, while free eyewitness memoirs exist.
- **Reliability:** about medium-high for the large-scale picture, medium for brigade detail and timings.

## Issues (severity · location · problem · evidence · fix)
1. **High · `col1` phase 7 at (872, 1170).**
   - *Problem:* the point lies inside the Satschan pond polygon, so 10,700 men stand on the ice. It contradicts the dyke narrative.
   - *Evidence:* Stutterheim pp. 122–3: Dokhturov collected his men "upon the plain between Tellnitz and the lake".
   - *Fix:* move the unit north-west of the pond and route it via the dyke.
2. **Medium · brief D1 and the `col1`/`col3` reasoning.**
   - *Problem:* the West Point figures are not a labelling error.
   - *Evidence:* they reproduce Stutterheim's earlier march-order table, before the columns were reorganised. In that table the 1st Column was 18 bns and 8,320 men, the 2nd 18 bns and 11,420, and the 3rd 24 bns and 13,800. By 1 December the brigades had been renumbered (Stutterheim, archive text lines 1530–1560 against 2120–2160). The data are right; the stated reasoning is wrong.
   - *Fix:* rewrite D1.
3. **Medium · phase 5 text and event.**
   - *Problem:* the narrative credits the eagle to the wrong arm.
   - *Evidence:* Fraser 1912, pp. 107–9; Wikipedia, "Life Guard Horse Regiment". The Russian Guard infantry drove back the 4e and 24e; the **Life Guard Horse** rode down 1/4e and took its eagle. The 24e's eagle was also lost but recovered on the field.
   - *Fix:* reword, and qualify "only eagle".
4. **Medium · `va3` note and the phase 7 event "Vandamme's guns above Aujezd".**
   - *Problem:* the fire on the ponds came from **French Guard artillery** placed by Napoleon, not Vandamme's guns.
   - *Evidence:* md1846 p. 277; Stutterheim p. 125; Marbot, vol. 1.
   - *Fix:* reattribute the fire to the Guard artillery and keep Vandamme as the force that took Aujezd.
5. **Medium · `kie2` routed over the dyke.**
   - *Problem:* the dyke was two men wide.
   - *Evidence:* Stutterheim: the Hessen-Homburg hussars went ahead to the heights between Satschan and Ottnitz, while the O'Reilly chevaulegers and Szekler hussars covered Dokhturov from the Telnitz–Menitz hill. Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky: Kienmayer went "round the lake".
   - *Fix:* route the unit around the Menitz pond.
6. **Medium · phase 7, "Langeron gets part of his column out through Aujezd".**
   - *Evidence:* Stutterheim p. 121 says it was Buxhöwden "with a few battalions", and that about 4,000 men were taken prisoner at Aujezd.
   - *Fix:* cite Stutterheim and add the prisoners.
7. **Medium · phase 2 timing and the Soult quote.**
   - *Evidence:* Ségur (present on the day) says 08:00 and "ten minutes"; Thiébault says "twenty minutes at most".
   - *Fix:* widen the debate to 08:00–09:00 and present the quote as reported, with both versions.
8. **Medium · Napoleon's afternoon position.**
   - *Problem:* after the Guard fight he stood at Staré Vinohrady, where Rapp presented the captured colours, not on the southern Pratzeberg.
   - *Evidence:* Fondation Napoléon places guide.
9. **Medium · sourcing.**
   - *Problem:* about 20 claims rest on warhistory.org, and the drainage finds rest on a popular article.
   - *Fix:* re-cite to Thiébault, Stutterheim, Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky or the scholarship, and mark claims that only warhistory supports (Langeron's "five of nine", "two to one", "≈200 drowned") as low.
10. **Medium · historiography.**
    - *Problem:* Duffy and Smith are used only through Wikipedia; Goetz (2005), Castle (2002), Chandler (1966), Bowden (1997) and the Mikaberidze translations are absent.
    - *Fix:* consult Duffy and Goetz directly, or note the gap.
11. **Low · losses.**
    - *Evidence:* Kutuzov's report gives 131 guns lost (Millar, Napoleon Series 2004); 12,000 of 27,000 prisoners is about 44%.
    - *Fix:* write "130–180 guns" and "nearly half prisoners".
12. **Low · `kie1` name.** It should read "Brod & Szekler Grenzer" ("Croats" is the period's generic word).
13. **Low · `fri1`.** The 15e Léger belongs to Kister's brigade. It should read "108e Ligne with the voltigeurs of the 15e Léger".
14. **Low · brief.** The 5th Column's Austrian squadrons are given as 32 (Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky), 40 (Stutterheim) and 20 (order of battle); the brief should state the range and the figure used.
15. **Low · `uhl`.** Name Essen II (who directed the charge) and Meller-Zakomelsky (the regiment's commander).
16. **Low · Kamensky's initiative.** Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky has Kamensky warning Langeron; Langeron's memoir claims the turn was his. Add a one-line debate.
17. **Low · missing event.** The Russian Guard Jäger defended Blasowitz against Lannes in the late morning.
18. **Low · phase 0.** "About 40,000 men in three columns" counts only about 32,200 without Kienmayer.
19. **Medium · confidence too high.**
    - Phase 0: the start time is disputed.
    - Phase 1: the timing rests on popular sources.
    - `col1` and `col3`: high only after the D1 reconciliation.
    - `leg2`: cited to Wikipedia alone.
    - *Fix:* lower all of these to medium.

## Verified independently
- Dokhturov's was the larger column (Stutterheim).
- The Life Guard Horse took the 4e Ligne's eagle (Fraser; Wikipedia).
- Soult's "twenty minutes" and the fog (Thiébault vol. 2, p. 153); Ségur gives 08:00 and "ten minutes".
- Thiébault and Morand fought Kamensky on the summit (Thiébault vol. 2, pp. 160–4).
- The Guard artillery fired on the ice by Napoleon's order, the dyke was two men wide, and the bridge broke (Marbot; Mikhailovsky-Danilevsky pp. 277–8).
- Napoleon was at the Zuran, then Staré Vinohrady, then St Anthony's chapel (Fondation Napoléon places guide).
- Davout's march fits the commonly cited 70 miles in 46 hours; no scholarly confirmation was found.
- The Allied strength range is 73,200–85,400, and Kutuzov reported 131 guns lost (Millar 2004).
- Oudinot was convalescent and Duroc commanded the grenadiers (order of battle).
