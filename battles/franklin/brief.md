# Franklin, 30 November 1864: research brief

## Overview
Hood's Army of Tennessee, having let Schofield's force slip past it at Spring Hill the night before, caught up with it at Franklin, Tennessee. There Schofield was entrenched in an arc south of the town, both flanks on the Harpeth River, while his trains crossed north towards Nashville. At about 4 p.m. Hood launched a frontal assault of some 18 infantry brigades across about two miles of open ground. It overran Wagner's two exposed forward brigades and broke into the main line at the Columbia Pike. Opdycke's reserve brigade and the reserve regiments of Reilly's brigade sealed the breach. The fighting at the works then continued in darkness until about 9 p.m. Schofield withdrew across the river around midnight.

## Phases (all 30 Nov 1864; sunset 4:59 p.m. local [cox])
0. **15:00 Hood deploys.** Wagner's brigades are dug in about 800 m south of the Carter House, Lane west of the pike and Conrad east of it, facing south, in line [jesp][cox]. The main line arcs from the Harpeth east of the railroad to the Carter's Creek Pike: Stiles, Casement, Reilly, Strickland, Moore, then Kimball's three brigades bending north to the river [jesp][cox]. Opdycke stands in reserve about 200 m north of the Carter House, in column [wp][jesp]. Wood's division is north of the river and Fort Granger's guns are 1.2 km NE of the Carter House [jesp][fw]. Stewart's corps deploys east of the pike, Cheatham's west of it, about 2.5 km south of the works below Winstead Hill [hood][jesp][fw]. Confidence: high.
1. **16:00 The advance.** A two-mile line steps off [abt][hood]. Forrest's Buford and Jackson have pushed Union cavalry back at Hughes's Ford, about 4.5 km ESE of the Carter House [wp]. Confidence: high. The step-off time is debated (see below).
2. **16:15 Wagner overrun.** Lane and Conrad give one volley and collapse. About 700 are captured and the rest run up the pike with Cleburne's and Brown's men on their heels ("Go into the works with them") [wp][cox]. The defenders cannot fire for fear of hitting their own men [abt]. Confidence: high.
3. **16:30 Breakthrough at the Carter House.** Cleburne, Brown and French converge on the pike. The 100th Ohio and Strickland's front are driven back and Confederates get about 50 yards into the line [wp]. Stewart's corps, squeezed between the railroad and the river, hits Stiles and Casement and stalls in the osage-orange abatis under fire from Fort Granger. Adams is killed on the parapet [wp]. Wilson's cavalry drives Forrest back across the Harpeth [wp][abt]. Confidence: high (sequence); medium (exact brigade geometry).
4. **16:45 Opdycke's counterattack.** Opdycke deploys astride the pike and charges into the breach. Reilly's reserve regiments (12th and 16th Kentucky, 175th Ohio) hold a second line. The line is restored and Confederates are pinned in the outer ditch [wp][abt][hood]. Cleburne and Granbury are killed near the cotton gin [wp][hood]. Confidence: high.
5. **17:00 Sunset; the flanks fail.** Bate attacks the Union right past Everbright without Chalmers's support and is repulsed. Chalmers's dismounted cavalry fails against Kimball [wp]. Repeated Confederate assaults at the centre fail, and both wings are beaten back [cox]. Confidence: medium.
6. **19:00 Johnson's division attacks.** Hood commits the only one of Lee's divisions to have arrived. It loses its alignment in the dark west of the pike, and its single assault is repulsed [wp][hood]. Confidence: medium.
7. **21:00 Firing dies down.** Effective fire ends around 9 p.m. [cox][hood], or about 10 p.m. [tnenc]. Men on each side lie a few feet apart across the parapet [tnenc]. Confidence: medium.
8. **23:00 Union withdrawal.** Schofield's order is issued at 7:15 p.m. and the withdrawal begins near midnight, over the railroad and pontoon bridges to Nashville [cox]. Hood says the enemy left about 3 a.m. [hood]. Confidence: medium.

## Disagreements and choices
- **Step-off time.** 4 p.m. [hood][abt][jesp] or 4:30 p.m. [tnenc]. **Chosen: 4:00.** Hood, Cox's narrative of the first hour and the modern map agree independently.
- **Confederate losses.** 6,252 [cox, Schofield's report] or 4,500 [hood]. Cox estimates about 8,000 including the lightly wounded. **Chosen: 6,252** for the chart. It rests on a count of Confederate graves and hospitals made when Union troops reoccupied Franklin on 18 December, and modern summaries use it [abt]. Hood's lower figure is shown in the debate note.
- **Confederate attacking strength.** About 20,000 [abt], about 23,000 [tnenc] or just under 24,000 [cox]. **Chosen: about 23,000 engaged**, spread across brigades as estimates. Only Cockrell's 696 is a sourced brigade figure [cox].
- **Who saved the centre.** Cox credits Reilly's reserves and Opdycke's own initiative. Stanley's partisans credit Stanley [wp]. Both counterattacks are shown. Opdycke acted on his own initiative before Stanley reached him [wp].
- **Hood's motive** (punishing the army for Spring Hill, or a rational last chance before Nashville). This is outside the map and only noted.

## Myths
- **Artillery withheld to spare the townspeople.** Hood says so [hood], but only two Confederate batteries were up and engaged in any case [cox]. Both are noted.
- **"Pine cones used as ammunition."** Unsourced (Wikipedia flags it "citation needed"). Omitted.
- **"Bigger than Pickett's Charge."** The comparison is to manpower and losses, not a measured fact. Omitted as a claim.

## Unit confidence
- High: Union brigades in the works (Jespersen plus the 1864 field-works map); Opdycke; Wagner's forward position.
- Medium: Confederate brigade frontages at contact; Forrest and Wilson around Hughes's Ford.
- Low: Johnson's brigades at 7 p.m.; Bate and Chalmers on the far left after dark; all Confederate brigade strengths except Cockrell's.
