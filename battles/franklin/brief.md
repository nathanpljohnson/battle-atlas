# Franklin, 30 November 1864: research brief

## Overview
Hood's Army of Tennessee, having let Schofield's force slip past it at Spring Hill the night before, caught up with it at Franklin, Tennessee. There Schofield was entrenched in an arc south of the town, both flanks on the Harpeth River, while his trains crossed north towards Nashville. At about 4 p.m. Hood launched a frontal assault of some 18 infantry brigades across about two miles of open ground. It overran Wagner's two exposed forward brigades and broke into the main line at the Columbia Pike. Opdycke's reserve brigade and the reserve regiments of Reilly's brigade sealed the breach. The fighting at the works then continued in darkness until about 9 p.m. Schofield withdrew across the river around midnight.

## Phases (all 30 Nov 1864; sunset 4:59 p.m. local [cox])
0. **15:00 Hood deploys.** Wagner's brigades are dug in about 450–650 m south of the Carter House (Stanley: 300 yards in front of the works [or]; Cox: half a mile [cox]), Lane west of the pike and Conrad east of it, facing south, in line [jesp][cox]. The main line arcs from the Harpeth east of the railroad to the Carter's Creek Pike: Stiles, Casement, Reilly, Strickland, Moore, then Kimball's three brigades bending north to the river [jesp][cox]. Opdycke stands in reserve about 200 m north of the Carter House, in column [wp][jesp]. Wood's division is north of the river and Fort Granger (Battery D, 1st Ohio) is about 1.4 km NE of the Carter House [jesp][fw]. Stewart's corps deploys east of the pike, Cheatham's west of it, about 2.5 km south of the works below Winstead Hill [hood][jesp][fw]. Confidence: high.
1. **16:00 The advance.** A two-mile line of about 20,000 steps off [abt][hood]. Buford fights dismounted on Stewart's right between the Lewisburg Pike and the river. Jackson crosses at Hughes's Ford, about 3.3 km SE of the Carter House (2–3 miles in the sources) [or][wp]. Confidence: medium. The step-off time is debated (see below).
2. **16:15 Wagner overrun.** Lane and Conrad give one volley and collapse. About 700 are captured and the rest run up the pike with Cleburne's and Brown's men on their heels ("Go into the works with them") [wp][cox]. The defenders cannot fire for fear of hitting their own men [abt]. Confidence: high.
3. **16:30 Breakthrough at the Carter House.** Cleburne, Brown and French converge on the pike. The 100th Ohio and Strickland's front are driven back and Confederates get about 50 yards into the line [wp]. Stewart's corps, squeezed between the railroad and the river, hits Stiles and Casement and stalls in the osage-orange abatis under fire from Fort Granger. Adams is killed on the parapet in front of Stiles's right and Casement's left [or][wp]. Cleburne is killed near the cotton gin [wp][hood]. Confidence: medium.
4. **16:45 Opdycke's counterattack.** Opdycke deploys astride the pike and charges into the breach. Reilly's reserve regiments (12th and 16th Kentucky, 175th Ohio) hold a second line. The 44th Missouri and 183rd Ohio hold the retrenchment west of the pike [wp]. The line is restored and Confederates are pinned in the outer ditch [wp][abt][hood]. Confidence: high.
5. **17:00 Sunset; the flanks fail.** Bate attacks the Union right past Everbright without Chalmers's support and is repulsed. Chalmers's dismounted cavalry fails against Kimball [wp]. Repeated Confederate assaults at the centre fail, and both wings are beaten back [cox]. Confidence: medium.
6. **18:00 Johnson's division attacks.** Hood commits the only one of Lee's divisions to have arrived. It loses its alignment in the dark west of the pike, and its single assault is repulsed. Lee says it struck about an hour after dark [or][wp]. Across the river Hatch drives Jackson back over the Harpeth by 7 p.m. [or]. Confidence: medium.
7. **21:00 Firing dies down.** Effective fire ends around 9 p.m. [cox][hood], or about 10 p.m. [tnenc]. Men on each side lie a few feet apart across the parapet [tnenc]. Confidence: medium.
8. **23:00 Union withdrawal.** Schofield issues his order between six and seven, and the Fourth Corps passes it on at 7:15 p.m. [cox]. The withdrawal begins near midnight, over the railroad and pontoon bridges to Nashville [cox]. Hood says the enemy left about 3 a.m. [hood]. Confidence: medium.

## Disagreements and choices
- **Step-off time.** 4 p.m. [hood][abt][jesp] or 4:30 p.m. [tnenc]. **Chosen: 4:00.** Hood, Cox's narrative of the first hour and the modern map agree independently.
- **Confederate losses.** 6,252 [cox, Schofield's report] or 4,500 [hood]. Cox estimates about 8,000 including the lightly wounded. **Chosen: 6,252** for the chart. It rests on a count of Confederate graves and hospitals made when Union troops reoccupied Franklin on 18 December, and modern summaries use it [abt]. Hood's lower figure is shown in the debate note.
- **Confederate attacking strength.** About 20,000 [abt], about 23,000 [tnenc] or just under 24,000 [cox]. **Chosen: about 20,000 in the main assault** [abt][wp], plus Johnson's roughly 3,300 after dark. The brigade estimates for Stewart and Cheatham sum to about 17,900, a little under that figure. Only Cockrell's 696 is a sourced brigade figure [cox][or].
- **Who saved the centre.** Cox credits Reilly's reserves and Opdycke's own initiative. Stanley's partisans credit Stanley [wp]. Both counterattacks are shown. Opdycke acted on his own initiative before Stanley reached him [wp].
- **Hood's motive** (punishing the army for Spring Hill, as Sword argues, or a rational last chance before Nashville, as Stephen M. Hood argues). This is outside the map and only noted.
- **Johnson's attack time.** "Just before dark" [hood], "one hour after dark" [or, Lee], or about 7 p.m. [wp]. **Chosen: about 6 p.m.**, following Lee, the division's corps commander, and Cox.
- **Wagner's distance forward.** 300 yards [or, Stanley] or half a mile [cox][abt]. **Chosen:** Jespersen's placement, about 450–650 m.

## Myths
- **Artillery withheld to spare the townspeople.** Hood says so [hood], but only two Confederate batteries were up and engaged in any case [cox]. Both are noted.
- **"Pine cones used as ammunition."** Unsourced (Wikipedia flags it "citation needed"). Omitted.
- **"Bigger than Pickett's Charge."** The comparison is to manpower and losses, not a measured fact. Omitted as a claim.

## Unit confidence
- High: Union brigades in the works (Jespersen plus the 1864 field-works map); Opdycke; Wagner's forward position.
- Medium: Confederate brigade frontages at contact; Forrest and Wilson around Hughes's Ford.
- Medium: Bate's brigades (positions from Jespersen, timing from narrative).
- Low: Johnson's brigades after dark; Chalmers; the two Confederate batteries; all Confederate brigade strengths except Cockrell's.
