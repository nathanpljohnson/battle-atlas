# Thermopylae 480 BC: source notes

## Primary
- `herodotus_macaulay.txt`: Herodotus 7.175-233, 8.21-25 (Macaulay 1890). The framework source. Key facts: the pass 'half a plethron' (~15 m) at its narrowest; a single cart-track at Alpenoi (east) and at the Phoinix/Anthela (west); hot springs and an altar of Heracles; the Phocian wall rebuilt (7.176). Herodotus's compass is rotated 90 degrees: his 'west' = south (mountain), 'east' = north (sea) (Godley, cited by Stronk 2015 p. 166). Greek list 7.202-203: 300 Spartans, 500 Tegea, 500 Mantinea, 120 Orchomenos, 1,000 other Arcadians, 400 Corinth, 200 Phlius, 80 Mycenae, 700 Thespiae, 400 Thebes, Opuntian Locrians 'in full force', 1,000 Phocians. Four days' wait, then day 1: Medes and Cissians, then the Immortals under Hydarnes; feigned flights (7.210-211). Day 2: the same; contingents fight in turn; the Phocians are on the mountain (7.212). Ephialtes; Hydarnes leaves 'about lamp-lighting' (7.215); the path begins at the Asopus gorge, runs along the ridge, and ends at Alpenos/Melampygos (7.216); Oetaean mountains on the right, Trachinian on the left; at dawn, at the summit; oak woods; Phocians flee to the peak; Persians descend (7.217-218). Night/dawn warnings; allies dismissed; Thespians (Demophilos) and Thebans (Leontiades) stay (7.219-222). Xerxes' libations at sunrise; he waits until 'the hour when the market fills'; the Greeks advance into the broader part; many Persians driven into the sea (7.223). Leonidas falls; Abrocomes and Hyperanthes fall; four rallies over the body (7.224-225). Retreat behind the wall to the hillock (kolonos) at the entrance, where the stone lion now is; overwhelmed by missiles (7.225). Thebans surrender (7.233). 4,000 Peloponnesians (epigram 7.228). Persian dead 20,000 (8.24); 4,000 Greek bodies incl. helots displayed (8.25).
- `diodorus_11_4-11.txt`: Diodorus 11.4-11 (Oldfather), via Ephorus. 1,000 Lacedaemonians + 300 Spartiates + 3,000 other Greeks; 1,000 Locrians, 1,000 'Melians' (Malians), ~1,000 Phocians, 400 Thebans (11.4.5-7). Day 1: Medes (with relatives of the Marathon dead), then Cissians and Sacae, then the Immortals at nightfall (11.6-7). Day 2: picked men from all peoples (11.8). A Trachinian guide; 20,000 men sent at night; Tyrrhastiadas of Cyme deserts to warn (11.8.4-5). Leonidas keeps only the Lacedaemonians and Thespians (~500) and makes a NIGHT attack on Xerxes' camp and tent (11.9-10). Rejected by most moderns in favour of Herodotus.
- `pausanias_10_20-22.txt`: Pausanias 10.20.1-2 repeats Herodotus's figures and estimates the Locrians at no more than 6,000 (total 11,200). 10.22.8: two paths over Oeta; Hydarnes used the easier one 'through the territory of the Aenianians'.
- `strabo_9_4.txt`: Strabo 9.4.13-17: Callidromus above the pass; Heracleia/Trachin; the Asopus, Melas and Dyras rivers.

## Modern scholarship
- `kv_atlas_djvu.txt` (text of Kromayer-Veith, Schlachten-Atlas zur antiken Kriegsgeschichte, Griech. Abt. Blatt 1, maps 5-7, text by J. Kromayer, 1922-29), resting on L. and E. Harmening, 'Thermopylae', in Kromayer, Antike Schlachtfelder IV (1924-31) 21 ff. Translated key points (German -> English):
  - 'The maps rest on Grundy's survey in The Great Persian War and on the Carte de la Grèce [1:50,000 original survey].'
  - 'Our sources: about 4,000 Peloponnesian hoplites, to which about as many light and heavy troops from central Greece may have been added. Beloch's estimate of over 20,000 goes too far in my view. Xerxes' army was many times superior, but we cannot say more precisely. The millions of our sources are worthless (Delbrück).'
  - 'The appearance of the pass is entirely changed by the alluvium of the Spercheios; where the sea was, a wide marsh plain now spreads. The ancient coast can be roughly restored from Herodotus 7.176 (the sea came right up to the rocks at three places) and the course of the contours, especially the 10 m line. This gives Herodotus's three narrows, marked West, Middle and East Gate.'
  - 'At the Middle Gate, where high rocks prevented direct outflanking, lay the Greek position, supported by the Phocian wall, which ran over hill 1. The fighting of the first two days took place in front of it; Leonidas's last sortie on day 3 carried the fight somewhat further west onto the wider ground. The rest of his force died behind the wall, probably on hill 2 [map 7 labels hill 3 Leonidashügel]. The Greek camp lay in the wider plain east of the wall; the Persian camp west of the West Gate.'
  - 'The outflanking went via Alt-Damasta, Alt-Drakospilia and the saddle of the Sastani ridge, where the Phocian detachment stood, then down the Alpenoi gorge to Alpenoi.' He reviews four other routes (Gordon 1838 via Kalkomata-Paleo-Eleftherochori-Nevropolis-Drakospilia; Leake via the Asopus gorge, Damasta and a long eastern descent by Mendenitsa; Grundy 1901 via the Panagia monastery and Lithitza; Munro 1902 via the west of Trachis). All of them except Munro cross the Sastani saddle. He rejects the longer ones as too long for a short summer night.
- `stronk_talanta.txt`: J. P. Stronk, 'Thermopylae 480 BC: ancient accounts of a battle', Talanta 46-47 (2014-15) 165-236. Peer-reviewed comparison of every ancient account. Points used: the sea is now 1-8 km further out than in 480 (Kraft et al. 1987; Rapp 2013; Vouvalidis et al. 2010 confirm Herodotus's description). The pass is a cart-way (2-3 m) at its ends and ~15 m at its narrowest; the Anopaea route is unsettled (Wallace 1980 vs Pritchett 1982; Green 1996 follows Pritchett). His Table I compares the Greek strengths: Herodotus 5,200 (or 6,100) plus the Locrians; Diodorus 7,400 (or 7,700). Date: probably August or September 480. Carneia and Olympics; the Immortals' 10,000 is Herodotean only.
- Kraft, Rapp, Szemler, Tziavos and Kase, 'The Pass at Thermopylae, Greece', Journal of Field Archaeology 14 (1987) 181-198 (JSTOR 530139). Not read directly. Via secondary summaries (Stronk; Rapp and Hill 2006 fig. 3.19, redrawn in shoreline_changes.png): the 480 BC shoreline lay close under the cliffs, and the Middle Gate was some 20-30 m wide (Pritchett thought this too wide).
- `standrews_kallidromos.txt`: Mountains of Greece blog (St Andrews, 2025). Near-consensus (Wallace 1980; Pritchett) that the central section of the path ran through the upper Asopus meadows from Eleftherochori to Nevropoli and Limni Kallidromou. The marked modern 'Anopaia' hiking trail via the Damasta monastery is 'almost certainly not' the route. Candidate Phocian hill: the height NE of the lake.

## Maps
- `kv_blatt1.jpg` (Kromayer-Veith Blatt 1, archive.org kromayerveithschlachtenatlas n134) -> `kv5.jpg` (1:100,000 overview: the 480 BC coast, Persian camp, five path hypotheses, Phocian position) and `kv6.jpg` (1:25,000: the West, Middle and East Gates, the Phocian wall, hills 1-3, the Greek camp; inset 7 at 1:12,500 shows the fighting areas of days 1-2 and day 3 and the last retreat).
- `mauro_maps01.jpg`: on-site panel at the Thermopylae monument (photo D. Mauro 2018, CC BY-SA). The probable coast in 480 BC, the Persian camp between the Melas and Asopus, Trachis, ancient Anthela, gates 1-3, the Phokian wall, the Greek camp, Alpenoi, and the Anopaia path (Asopus gorge -> south over the plateau -> east -> north down to the 3rd gate/Alpenoi). In Greek and English.
- `mauro_maps02.jpg`: the same panel. Day 1-2 phases (initial fight, tactical retreat, counter-attack at the wall) and day 3 (Leonidas's death at position b, west of the wall; last stand ringed on Kolonos).
- `mauro_battle.jpg`: Thermopylae Battle Museum (Kentro Istorias Thermopylon) panel, bilingual (photo D. Mauro 2018). The Persian camp on the Asopus-Melas plain, the Anopaia path from the Asopus gorge over Kallidromos, the descent east of Kolonos; A = days 1-2 in front of the wall, Γ = day 3 sortie further west, Δ = the end on Kolonos.
- `shoreline_changes.png`, `map480_chriso.png`: schematic redraw (ChrisO, Wikimedia 2008) after Rapp and Hill, Geoarchaeology (2006) fig. 3.19 (from Kraft et al. 1987). Shorelines of 2500 BC, 480 BC, 1876 and today. Only a schematic scale bar.
- `usma_salamis.gif`: USMA strategic map (Thermopylae to Salamis). Strategic scale only.
- `coast_photo.jpg`: photo from near the Phocian wall area (Fkerasar 2011). Wikimedia caption: the ancient coastline ran where the modern road lies, or closer to the mountain.

## Narrative
- `wp_battle_of_thermopylae.txt`, `wp_kolonos.txt`: Wikipedia (Lazenby, Holland, Green, Cartledge, de Souza as cited there). Modern estimates of the Persian army 120,000-300,000; Greek ~7,000 standard. Kolonos at 38.79598 N 22.53905 E (Marinatos 1939 arrowhead finds).
