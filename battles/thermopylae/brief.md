# Thermopylae, 480 BC: research brief

## Scope and method

This is a three-day reconstruction: "day 1" is the first day of fighting, after Xerxes had waited four days [hdt]. Herodotus 7.175-233 is the framework. He is the earliest account, he knew or interviewed people who knew the site, and modern topographers (Grundy, Kromayer, Pritchett) find his features identifiable on the ground [hdt] [stronk]. Diodorus (after Ephorus) is the main counter-account and the source of different strengths [diod]. Isocrates confirms Diodorus's 1,000 Lacedaemonians [isoc]. Pausanias adds a Locrian estimate and a second view of the path [paus]. Rop (2019) is the most recent study used directly: it covers the Phocian post, the path and the date [rop].

The ground is the hard part. The Spercheios and the torrents off Kallidromos have pushed the shore of the Malian Gulf 1-8 km out since 480 BC [stronk]. The pass that was 20-30 m wide is now the edge of a plain several kilometres across [reed]. The map therefore draws the **480 BC sea** as a water body over today's plain, and lowers the ground inside it to sea level. Its line comes from the georeferenced Kromayer-Veith maps at 1:100,000 and 1:25,000. They reconstruct it from Herodotus's three narrows and the 10 m contour of Grundy's 1899 survey and the French staff survey [kv] [grundy]. Grundy independently put the 480 BC shore at about the 5-10 yard contour [grundy].

**After the review**, the Middle Gate was redrawn to follow the borehole study of Kraft et al. (1987): the pass was "not more than 20-30 meters wide" [reed] [kraft]. Kromayer's line there gave a strip 100-200 m wide. The shore is now 40-60 m from the cliff foot, measured on the DEM, for about 700 m west of the wall (the narrows). It then opens round Kolonos, which must have been dry land because it was the site of the last stand. The pass floor at the Middle Gate is lowered from today's 20-35 m to a schematic 6 m. The present surface there is largely later travertine from the hot springs and alluvium, up to about 20 m deep [stronk] [reed]. The true 480 BC level is not known.

## Overview: strengths and losses (ranges)

- **Greeks.** Herodotus 7.202-203 lists 3,100 Peloponnesians, 700 Thespians, 400 Thebans, 1,000 Phocians, and the Opuntian Locrians "in full force" [hdt]. The Peloponnesians are 300 Spartans, 500 Tegeans, 500 Mantineans, 120 Orchomenians, 1,000 other Arcadians, 400 Corinthians, 200 from Phlius and 80 from Mycenae.
  - The epigram gives 4,000 Peloponnesians [hdt]. Diodorus has 1,000 Lacedaemonians besides the 300 Spartiates [diod], and so does Isocrates ("a thousand picked soldiers of their own") [isoc]. Most moderns read these as perioikoi, which reconciles 3,100 with 4,000 [stronk].
  - Diodorus also has 1,000 Locrians, 1,000 Malians (not shown: no other source puts them at the pass), about 1,000 Phocians and 400 Thebans [diod]. Pausanias puts the Locrians at no more than 6,000 [paus].
  - **Range: about 5,200 to 11,200.** The atlas models **8,100**: Herodotus's list + 1,000 Lacedaemonians + 900 helots (a modern inference from the helots among the dead, 8.25) + 1,000 Locrians (Diodorus's figure, the most conservative) [hdt] [diod] [isoc] [wp].
- **Persians.** Herodotus's 1.7 million infantry is impossible. Modern estimates for the whole army mostly run from 120,000 to 300,000, with a few lower [wp] [kv]. The atlas uses **120,000** as a commonly cited round figure, not as a derived total. Only the troops Herodotus names are shown as separate blocks: the Medes, the Cissians, the Immortals (10,000, a Herodotean figure [stronk]) and the troops driven forward on day 3. The rest is a camp block.
- **Losses.** Herodotus gives 20,000 Persian dead (8.24) and 4,000 Greek bodies, including helots (8.25) [hdt]. Modern writers usually accept roughly 2,000 Greek dead [wp]. The atlas allocates 20,000 Persian dead across phases. On the Greek side it shows about 2,100 dead and about 340 Thebans captured: 8,100 at the start and 5,660 who leave or survive. The per-phase figures are estimates.

## Disagreements and the readings chosen

1. **Herodotus vs Diodorus on the last day (chosen: Herodotus).** Diodorus has a night attack on Xerxes' camp and tent by about 500 men [diod]. Herodotus has a daylight sortie into the wider part of the pass and a last stand on the kolonos [hdt]. Herodotus is earlier, closer to eyewitnesses, and agrees with the arrowheads on Kolonos [brown]. Stronk treats the Diodoran version as a later elaboration [stronk].
2. **The Phocian post (chosen: the hill by Nevropoli).** Pritchett (1958, 1982), MacKay (1963) and Wallace (1980) put it there, according to Rop n. 26 [rop]. Kromayer put it on the Sastani saddle above Drakospilia, and Reed's summary has the Persians reach "the peak of Sastano" at dawn [kv] [reed]. Chosen because it is the majority modern view, and because it makes Herodotus's timetable work (a dawn encounter on the plateau). Low confidence.
3. **The Anopaea route (chosen: the Kalkomata ascent, then the Nevropoli meadows, Alt-Drakospilia, the Sastani saddle and the Alpenoi ravine).** Herodotus fixes only the ends: "from the river Asopos, which flows through the cleft" to Alpenos [hdt].
   - Pritchett preferred the Kalkomata ascent; Wallace, Myres and Burn preferred Vardates [reed].
   - Kromayer argued for a shorter northern line via Alt-Damasta [kv].
   - Pausanias says the easier path "through the territory of the Aenianians" [paus].
   - There is near agreement on the meadow section [standrews] [rop]. Low confidence.
4. **Greek numbers (chosen: 8,100).** See Overview. The 1,000 Lacedaemonians were added after the review. Shown leaving with the allies on day 3, but whether they stayed is unknown [diod] [isoc].
5. **Persian numbers (chosen: 120,000 in the theatre; only the named waves engaged).** Herodotus's numbers are discarded, as every modern study does [kv] [wp].
6. **Who stayed (chosen: Herodotus).** 300 Spartans (less the absentees), 700 Thespians, and 400 Thebans held as hostages [hdt]. Diodorus has only the Lacedaemonians and Thespians, about 500 [diod]. The helots are assumed to have stayed; some were among the dead [hdt 8.25].
7. **The kolonos.** Before Marinatos, the last-stand hill was usually taken to be hill 1 by the wall. His arrowheads identify it with hill 2, today's Kolonos, which is also Grundy's "second mound" and Kromayer's "probably on hill 2" [reed] [grundy] [kv].
8. **Why the Phocians failed.** Herodotus: surprise and confusion [hdt]. Rop argues that the Persians knew the path on arrival, and that the Phocians, as Delphians, stood aside deliberately [rop]. Recorded in the phase 4 and 6 debates; not decided.
9. **The date (chosen: about 20 August).** Most scholars favour August (How and Wells, Burn, Hignett, Lazenby); Sacks and Hammond argue for mid-September [rop]. The sun is set for declination +12.5°: first light about 04:50, sunrise about 05:20 local solar time.
10. **Clock times.** Herodotus gives lamp-lighting, dawn, sunrise libations and "the hour the market fills" [hdt]. The 10:00 and 16:00 on day 1, 10:00 on day 2 and 01:00 on day 3 are illustrative spacing.

## Myths that modern scholarship rejects

- **"300 against a million."** The Greeks numbered roughly 5,000-11,000 throughout. Even on the last day the Spartans had the Thespians, the Thebans and the helots with them [hdt] [stronk]. The Persians were perhaps a tenth of Herodotus's figures [kv] [wp].
- **"A pass between a cliff and the sea, as today."** The modern visitor sees a wide plain; in 480 the sea came within a few tens of metres of the cliffs at the gates [kv] [stronk] [kraft].
- **"Ephialtes showed an unknown secret path."** Herodotus himself says the Malians had used it against the Phocians long before, and the Greeks learned of it on arrival and posted the Phocians [hdt 7.175, 7.215]. Rop argues that the Persians knew of it too [rop].
- **"Leonidas died on Kolonos."** He fell in the sortie in the wider ground W of the narrows; only the survivors reached the hill [hdt 7.224-225].

## Confidence summary

- High: Kolonos as the last-stand site; the sequence of the days; the Thebans' surrender.
- Medium: the 480 BC shore at the pass (the Kromayer line, with the Middle Gate pulled in to Kraft's width); the fighting areas of days 1-3; the camp areas; the Greek contingent sizes. W of the Asopus the shore is inferred.
- Low: the Anopaea route and the Phocian post; the allies' route of withdrawal; the Persian contingent strengths; the per-phase losses; the illustrative clock times; the 6 m floor of the pass.

## Places and georeferencing

- The gazetteer flags are resolved in places.json.
  - **Damasta:** Wikidata gave the Damasta monastery; OSM gave Ano Damasta, 2 km from both of Kromayer's Damastas. The monastery is used as a control point (Kromayer's Kloster Panagia); Ano Damasta is dropped.
  - **Drakospilia:** the modern village = Kromayer's Ober-Drakospilia (28 m residual).
  - **Molos and Alamana:** taken from OSM.
- Map 5 fits seven points at 173 m. Seven others were dropped as relocated villages or ancient sites (see maps.json).
- Map 6 fits OSM's Phocian wall, Kolonos and the old Thermopylae baths at 25 m. Anthele is a label centre with a 431 m leave-one-out error, so its weight is low. These points are control points, not an independent check.
- OSM's "Akropolis Alpinon" sits at 537 m on the mountain, which is not Grundy's hill on the plain, so Kromayer's Alpenoi is used instead.

## Terrain and modern change

- **Grid:** 520 × 359 cells (43 m); vertical exaggeration 1.5.
- **Masks:** OSM quarries are auto-masked. The auto-masks on the Nevropoli and Paliosouvala lakes were removed: they are natural karst lakes on the plateau, not flooded pits.
- **The sea:** the 480 BC sea (`seas`, level −2 m) covers the modern plain. The A1 motorway embankments inside it disappear with it.
- **The pass floor:** at the Middle Gate it is lowered to 6 m (see Scope).
- **Woods:** the oak woods on Kallidromos are schematic, after Herodotus 7.218.
- **No overlay:** no historical-map overlay is offered, per Nathan's standing preference.

## Review responses

The independent review (review.md) raised 16 issues.

1. **The Phocian post and the route are inconsistent. Fixed.** The Pritchett/MacKay/Wallace scheme is now used throughout: the post on the hill by Nevropoli (DEM 1,125 m, 550 m NE of the lake), the dawn encounter there at 05:00, then east by Alt-Drakospilia, the saddle and the Alpenoi ravine. The source is Rop n. 26, read directly. Kromayer's saddle is kept in the debate.
2. **The pass width and the raised strip. Fixed.** The Middle Gate shore is pulled in to 40-60 m from the DEM cliff foot over the 700 m of narrows W of the wall. Kolonos is kept on land, and the wall is shortened to about 170 m across the strip. The pass floor is lowered to a schematic 6 m and the note is stated in method and brief. The shore at the pass is downgraded to medium. Vouvalidis et al. 2010 is still unread.
3. **The 1,000 Lacedaemonians. Fixed.** A unit "Lacedaemonians (perioikoi?)" is added (Diodorus, Isocrates read directly; low confidence). The helot unit is renamed "Helots". The total is now 8,100, and the Malians are mentioned.
4. **The phase 3 misattribution. Fixed.** Diodorus's picked men are "threatened with death … reserves blocking their retreat". His "would not withdraw" (no rotation) is added to the debate.
5. **"Wicker shields." Fixed.** The text now quotes Diodorus's "small round or irregularly shaped shields".
6. **The summit was lower than the saddle. Fixed.** Under the new scheme the Phocians fall back SE to the nearest higher summit (DEM 1,301 m), off the Persian line of march.
7. **Internal inconsistencies. Fixed.** Brief and data are reconciled: wall–Kolonos about 130 m (wall midpoint; OSM wall site 190 m), Kolonos coordinates from OSM/Wikidata, route lengths, times, 2,100 dead + 340 captured, and the sortie 970 m W.
8. **Sunrise and march pace. Fixed.** The sun is set for 20 August. Dawn is now 05:00, the dismissal 06:30 (after the 05:20 sunrise libations) and the arrival at Kolonos 11:30. Paces are 1.0, 1.9, 2.1 and 2.4 km/h.
9. **Historiography. Fixed in part.**
   - Rop's argument (Persian foreknowledge; a Phocian betrayal) and Herodotus's rival informers are now in the phase 4 and 6 debates.
   - The Kalkomata (Pritchett) and Vardates (Wallace) ascents are both given.
   - **Open:** Lazenby 1993, Green 1996, Cartledge 2006, Matthew and Trundle 2013, Kraft 1987 and Vouvalidis 2010 have still not been read directly. They are not freely available here, and Stronk 2015 and Rop 2019 (both read) summarise their positions.
10. **The arrowhead count. Fixed.** "About 100 bronze and iron arrowheads and a few spearheads", cited to Brown via Manning.
11. **The date. Fixed.** The sun is set for about 20 August, citing the majority-August view and Sacks/Hammond via Rop.
12. **"The low end of modern estimates." Fixed.** Reworded to "a commonly cited modern figure", with the range given; the unsupported water/supply claim is dropped.
13. **"Where it flows out of its gorge." Fixed.** Herodotus is now quoted; the gloss is left to the debate.
14. **"Checked against" wording. Fixed.** The method now says "georeferenced to"; the Anthele weight and the Ober-Drakospilia match are recorded under Places.

16. **Confidence labels. Fixed.** The shore at the pass is medium; the Immortals unit is low (its mountain phases), with a note that its positions in the pass are medium.
