# Thermopylae, 480 BC: research brief

## Scope and method

This is a three-day reconstruction (the first day of fighting is "day 1"; Xerxes had already waited four days [hdt]). Herodotus 7.175-233 is the framework because he is the earliest account, he visited or interviewed people who knew the site, and modern topographers (Grundy, Kromayer, Pritchett) find his features identifiable on the ground [hdt] [stronk]. Diodorus (after Ephorus) is used as the main counter-account and for his different strengths [diod]. Pausanias adds a Locrian estimate and a second view of the path [paus].

The ground is the hard part. The Spercheios and the torrents off Kallidromos have pushed the shore of the Malian Gulf 1-8 km out since 480 BC [stronk]; the pass that was 15-30 m wide is now the edge of a plain several kilometres wide. The map therefore draws the **480 BC sea** as a water body over today's plain. Its line is taken from the georeferenced Kromayer-Veith 1:100,000 and 1:25,000 maps, which reconstruct it from Herodotus's three narrows and the 10 m contour of Grundy's 1899 survey and the French staff survey [kv] [grundy]. Grundy independently put the 480 BC shore at about the 5-10 yard contour [grundy]. The line is checked against the geoarchaeological sketch after Kraft et al. 1987 / Rapp and Hill 2006 [kraft] and the two on-site museum panels [panel] [museum].

## Overview: strengths and losses (ranges)

- **Greeks.** Herodotus 7.202-203 lists 3,100 Peloponnesians (300 Spartans, 500 Tegeans, 500 Mantineans, 120 Orchomenians, 1,000 other Arcadians, 400 Corinthians, 200 from Phlius, 80 from Mycenae), 700 Thespians, 400 Thebans, 1,000 Phocians and the Opuntian Locrians "in full force" [hdt]. The epigram gives 4,000 Peloponnesians [hdt]. Diodorus gives 1,000 Lacedaemonians plus 300 Spartiates, 3,000 other Greeks, 1,000 Locrians, 1,000 Malians, about 1,000 Phocians and 400 Thebans: about 7,400 [diod]. Pausanias puts the Locrians at no more than 6,000, for 11,200 [paus]. Kromayer suggests about 4,000 Peloponnesian hoplites plus about as many central Greeks, and rejects Beloch's 20,000+ [kv]. **Range: about 5,200 to 11,200.** The atlas models **7,100**: Herodotus's list, plus 900 helots (the usual modern inference from the helots among the dead, 8.25) and 1,000 Locrians (Diodorus's figure, the most conservative) [hdt] [diod] [wp].
- **Persians.** Herodotus's 1.7 million infantry is impossible; modern estimates for the whole army run from about 120,000 to 300,000 [wp] [kv]. The atlas models **120,000**, the low end of that range, because only the low end fits the Malian plain's water and supply. Only the troops Herodotus names are shown as separate blocks: the Medes, the Cissians, the Immortals (10,000, a Herodotean figure [stronk]) and the troops driven forward on day 3. The rest is shown as a camp block.
- **Losses.** Herodotus gives 20,000 Persian dead (8.24) and shows 4,000 Greek bodies, including helots (8.25) [hdt]. Modern writers usually accept roughly 2,000 Greek dead on the last day plus earlier losses [wp]; the Persian figure cannot be checked. The atlas allocates 20,000 Persian dead across phases and about 2,700 Greek dead or captured; the per-phase figures are estimates.

## Disagreements and the readings chosen

1. **Herodotus vs Diodorus on the last day (chosen: Herodotus).** Diodorus has a night attack on Xerxes' camp and tent by about 500 men [diod]. Herodotus has a daylight sortie into the wider part of the pass and a last stand on the kolonos [hdt]. Herodotus is earlier, closer to eyewitnesses, and agrees with the archaeology: the mass of arrowheads on Kolonos (Marinatos 1939) [wp]. Stronk treats the Diodoran version as a later literary elaboration [stronk]. Diodorus is shown in the phase 8 debate note.
2. **The Anopaea route (chosen: the ascent from the Asopus gorge; the upper Asopus meadows Eleftherochori–Nevropoli; a descent past Drakospilia down the Alpenoi ravine).** Herodotus fixes only the ends: from the Asopus gorge to Alpenos [hdt]. Kromayer-Veith map five hypotheses. Kromayer argues for a short northern line (Alt-Damasta → Alt-Drakospilia → Sastani saddle) because the others are too long for a summer night [kv]. Wallace (1980) and Pritchett (1982) put the central section in the upper Asopus meadows; there is near agreement on that, and the modern signed trail via the Damasta monastery is unlikely [standrews] [stronk]. This reconstruction follows the meadow consensus for the middle (Gordon's line on Kromayer's map) and Kromayer's saddle and ravine for the descent, where every hypothesis but Munro's converges [kv]. That route is about 20 km; 10 hours of darkness makes it about 2 km/h, which is plausible for a column on a path. **The route is low confidence.**
3. **The Phocian post (chosen: Sastani saddle above Drakospilia, per Kromayer).** The alternatives are the hill NE of Limni Kallidromou [standrews] and the 'summit' of Herodotus 7.217 placed elsewhere. Low confidence.
4. **Greek numbers (chosen: 7,100).** See Overview. The Locrians (1,000 by Diodorus, up to 6,000 by Pausanias) and the helots are the main unknowns [diod] [paus].
5. **Persian numbers (chosen: 120,000 in the theatre; only the named waves engaged).** Herodotus's numbers are discarded, as every modern study does [kv] [wp].
6. **Who stayed (chosen: Herodotus).** Herodotus has 300 Spartans (less the absentees), the 700 Thespians and the 400 Thebans, the Thebans held as hostages [hdt]. Diodorus has only the Lacedaemonians and Thespians, about 500 [diod]. Helots are assumed to have stayed with the Spartans (some were among the dead [hdt 8.25]).
7. **The Phocian wall and the last-stand hill.** Kromayer puts the wall over his "hill 1" at the Middle Gate, with the end "probably on hill 2", and labels hill 3 the Leonidashügel [kv]. Grundy puts the stand on the "second mound" [grundy]. Marinatos's 1939 arrowheads fixed the kolonos as today's Kolonos hill [wp]. The atlas places the wall about 400 m W of Kolonos, where the Kromayer 1:25,000 map draws it.
8. **The date (chosen: about late August, with the sun as for 1 September).** Herodotus ties the campaign to the Carneia and the Olympic games; scholars put the battle in August or September 480 [hdt] [stronk]. The sun's declination is set for about 1 September (+8°).
9. **Clock times.** Herodotus gives lamp-lighting (Hydarnes' departure), dawn (the summit), sunrise libations and "the hour the market fills" (mid-morning) [hdt]. The other times (10:00 and 16:00 on day 1, 10:00 on day 2, 01:00 on day 3) are illustrative spacing.

## Myths that modern scholarship rejects

- **"300 against a million."** The Greeks were about 5,000-7,000+ throughout; even on the last day the Spartans had the Thespians, the Thebans and the helots with them [hdt] [stronk]. The Persians were perhaps a tenth of Herodotus's figures [kv] [wp].
- **"A pass between a cliff and the sea, as today."** The modern visitor sees a wide plain; in 480 the sea came to within metres of the cliffs at the three gates [kv] [stronk] [kraft].
- **"Ephialtes showed an unknown secret path."** Herodotus says the Malians had used it against the Phocians long before, and the Greeks learned of it on arrival and posted the Phocians [hdt 7.175, 7.215].
- **"Leonidas died on Kolonos."** He fell in the sortie in the wider ground W of the narrows; only the survivors reached the hill [hdt 7.224-225].

## Confidence summary

- High: the Middle Gate and Kolonos as the main fighting places; the sequence of days; the Thebans' surrender; the general position of the 480 BC shore at the pass.
- Medium: the camp areas; the fighting areas of days 1-3; the Greek contingent sizes; the 480 BC shore away from the pass (W of the Asopus, it is inferred).
- Low: the Anopaea route and the Phocian post; the allies' route of withdrawal; the Persian contingent strengths; the per-phase losses; the illustrative clock times.
