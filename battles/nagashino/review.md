# Independent review (general-purpose reviewer agent, 2026-10-16)

## Verdict

Not fit to publish yet. Most of the work is sound and readable after a moderate revision. The order of battle follows the 1903 staff history closely, and the camp, grave and palisade positions match the Shinshiro markers and the terrain. Shinchō kōki is quoted accurately, including the five waves, the 1,000 guns under five commanders, Sakai at the hour of the dog, the fighting to the hour of the sheep and Baba's "無比類". The Kōyō gunkan details on Baba are also faithful: 700 men, about 80 left, unwounded, named himself and did not draw. The page has five structural problems:

- **Tobigasu timing.** The page sets the fall of Tobigasu at 08:00 and cites the 1903 history for it. That history, and ja.wikipedia, actually put it at dawn, before the main attack.
- **Baba's death place.** It is misread throughout: 出沢 is *Suzawa*, not "Idesawa".
- **Tokugawa losses.** The allied side is said to have lost no senior commander. Matsudaira Koretada was killed, and the author's own source says so.
- **Guns.** Shinchō's 1,000 guns under the five commanders is treated as the army's total.
- **Modern scholarship.** It is cited only second-hand through Wikipedia, and the Baba article used there is itself flagged as unsourced.

Overall reliability: medium for positions and order of battle, low to medium for the timeline and the featured Baba thread.

## Issues

1. **High. Phase 4 (08:00 "Tobigasu"), brief phase 4, and the order of phases 2→3→4.**
   - **Problem:** Phase 4 puts the fall of Tobigasu after Yamagata's attack (05:30) and Baba's capture of Maruyama (06:30), and cites [shincho] and [nsenshi] for it. Only Shinchō gives 辰刻 (about 07:00–09:00), and Shinchō says that was when Sakai's men arrived and raised their banners (辰刻取上旗首を推立), not when the forts fell. The 1903 history says the opposite: Sakai attacked at 二十一日拂曉 (first light). At 平明 the Takeda "heard war cries towards Nagashino and saw smoke on Tobigasu", and only then did Katsuyori order the general attack (nihonsenshi_ocr p16–17). ja.wikipedia agrees: the attack came 翌日の夜明け, and the main battle began when Tobigasu was already decided (wp_ja_clean.txt l.59, l.66).
   - **Internal contradiction:** the Sakai unit note itself says "at dawn".
   - **Fix:** Make this a declared disagreement. Either move Tobigasu to dawn as the trigger for the attack (nsenshi and most modern narratives), or keep Shinchō's 辰刻 and say that the 1903 history disagrees. Remove [nsenshi] from the 08:00 claim, and drop phase 4's confidence from "high" to "medium".

2. **High. "Idesawa" everywhere** (place name, crossing, phase 9 title, brief, debate text).
   - **Problem:** 出沢 in Shinshiro is read **Suzawa (スザワ)**. Japan Post lists 出沢 スザワ 441-1313 (https://www.post.japanpost.jp/cgi-zip/zipcode.php?pref=23&city=1232210), and ja.wikipedia's Baba article glosses it 出沢（すざわ）.
   - **Fix:** Rename to Suzawa everywhere.

3. **High. Brief "Killed: … no senior commander lost [wpja]"; strengthNote.**
   - **Problem:** Matsudaira Koretada (深溝松平伊忠), a Tokugawa lord in Sakai's column, was killed in the Tobigasu fighting by Oyamada's counter-attack. This is in nsenshi p18 (伊忠…遂ニ之ニ死ス) and in the author's own wpja (l.61). It is confirmed independently by Nihon Daihyakka Zensho on Matsudaira Ietada: 長篠合戦のおりに父が戦死 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%BE%E5%B9%B3%E5%AE%B6%E5%BF%A0-136638).
   - **Allied loss figure:** The 6,000 allied losses are attributed to "modern summaries (poorly sourced)". They actually come from nsenshi p18 (死傷モ亦…六千ヲ下ヲス, i.e. killed *and wounded*).
   - **Fix:** Add Koretada's death as an event in phase 4 or 5, and attribute the 6,000 figure correctly as killed and wounded, from the 1903 history.

4. **Medium. Guns: brief Disagreement 5, myth 1, the gunners note ("~1,000 guns … ~1,000 (+500)").**
   - **Problem:** Shinchō's 千挺計 refers only to the guns placed under the five bugyō. It does not count the guns of the units' own contingents, the Tokugawa guns, or the guns Nobunaga levied from Hosokawa and Tsutsui. The author's own wpja (l.171) calls 1,500 "the minimum conceivable", and wpen (l.74) says "more than 1,500".
   - **Fix:** Keep the 1,000-strong five-commander detachment, but state that the army's total is unknown and was probably well over 1,500. Do not present 1,000 (+500) as the "chosen" total.

5. **Medium. Myth 2: "the Takeda … had about as many guns as their enemies".**
   - **Problem:** This cherry-picks wpen l.74/82. The same article (l.86–87, summarising the lead-isotope analysis of bullets and Hirayama) concludes the Takeda were outgunned and short of lead and powder.
   - **Fix:** Present it as disputed, with the ammunition-shortage evidence given.

6. **Medium. Units `watch` and `tobigasu`, and Annaka's unit "Annaka Kageshige, Wada and the West Kōzuke men".**
   - **Problem:** wpja (l.61) lists Wada Narishige, Saegusa Masasada, Gomi and Nawa Murisuke as killed at **Tobigasu**, not at Shitaragahara. Kofu city independently confirms Saegusa died at Tobigasu "with the castle-watch unit … caught in a pincer attack" (https://www.city.kofu.yamanashi.jp/welcome/rekishi/24generals/saigusa-masasada.html). Kōyō puts the named commanders Nawa, Ii and Gomi at Tobigasu.
   - **Fix:** Move Wada to Tobigasu, or flag the conflict with nsenshi. Name Saegusa in the Tobigasu note.

7. **Medium. Phase 5 narrative ("Sakai's column and the garrison drive the castle watch from Arumi [nsenshi]") and the `watch` unit moving north at phases 5–6.**
   - **Problem:** nsenshi p18 says the watch burned its camp and withdrew towards Ōno and Kadoya only after Tobigasu fell *and* news came of the defeat at Shitaragahara, which is after about 13:00. Okudaira then pursued it and killed Kōsaka Masazumi and about 200 men. wpja instead has Sakai sweeping Arumi.
   - **Fix:** Cite the right source for each version, or move the rout to phase 7–8. Also note that Kōsaka's traditional grave is at Shitaragahara (OSM 34.9095, 137.5198), not at Arumi.

8. **Medium. Baba thread, phase 3 and the Baba unit note: "took the Maruyama knoll and halted there … [koyo]"; event "Baba's 700 take Maruyama [koyo]".**
   - **Problem:** Kōyō ch. 52 never mentions Maruyama, the halt, or any order of events. It lists Baba first in an enumeration of where the Takeda won (一戦には皆武田方勝申候, 仔細は…). Maruyama and the halt come only from the 1903 history, which drew on Edo compilations, and from ja.wikipedia's Baba article. That article is tagged {{出典の明記}} (no sources, since 2017).
   - **Fix:** Cite the halt on Maruyama to [nsenshi] alone. Reword Disagreement 2 to say that Kōyō does not give a sequence. Lower the Baba-specific events in phases 3 and 5 to low–medium.

9. **Medium. Phase 9 debate and brief Disagreement 3 (Suzawa against "the Nagashino bridge site").**
   - **Problem:** The "Nagashino bridge site" crossing is placed beside the castle (34.9218, 137.5585) without a source. nsenshi p18 has Baba turn at 猿橋 (Saruhashi) and stand on the knoll at Suzawa. Kōyō's 長篠橋場 may well be the same Kansa-river crossing on the retreat route, so the disagreement may be an artefact of where the author put the crossing.
   - **Grave:** The grave by the castle is traditionally a head-mound (首塚), not a death site (tabi-mag ai0571). The 1740 *Mikawa no kuni futabamatsu* gives 於出沢之沢尻討死, a third witness for Suzawa.
   - **Fix:** Remove the castle-side "bridge site" crossing, or source it. Describe the grave as a head-mound.

10. **Medium. Event in phase 8: "Kasai Higo killed here … (marker) [sites]" at (34.94613, 137.54456).**
    - **Problem:** No such marker exists in osm_sites.json, osm2.json or osm_cache.json, and I could not find one on the web.
    - **Fix:** Drop [sites] and "(marker)". Place the event loosely: Kōyō says only that Kasai "rode back about one chō" and was killed.

11. **Medium. Historiography in general (brief, Sources table).**
    - **Problem:** Takayanagi (1960), Fujimoto, Suzuki, Hirayama (2014) and Kaneko (2018/2023) are cited only through ja.wikipedia. None of them was read. Two key Tokugawa-side primary sources are missing:
      - *Mikawa monogatari*, by Ōkubo Tadataka, himself present and the brother of the Ōkubo pair who fought Yamagata.
      - Ieyasu's letter of 5.19 on building the palisade.
    - **Missing argument:** The newer argument that most senior Takeda deaths came in the retreat rather than at the palisade (wpen l.78) is not mentioned. The map kills Sanada, Tsuchiya and Hara at the palisade on Kōyō's authority alone.
    - **Fix:** Consult at least Hirayama 2014 and Kaneko 2023 directly, and add a debate note on where the deaths happened.

12. **Low. Forces block:** `start` gives Allies 40,500 (Oda 0.765). The units actually sum to Oda 30,000 and Tokugawa 9,500, which is 39,500 (checked in nagashino.json). Fix the numbers.

13. **Low. Units `sakai` and `kanamori`:**
    - Shinchō says about 2,000 Tokugawa troops plus 500 Oda guns and inspectors, about 4,000 in all. The map gives 3,000 and 1,000.
    - The Sakai note says "About 2,000 picked …" but the unit shows 3,000.
    - nsenshi gives about 3,000.
    - **Fix:** Reconcile the figures, and lower Sakai's confidence from "high" to "medium".

14. **Low. Phase 1 and the brief: "Nobunaga has moved to Chausuyama [shincho]".** Shinchō has Nobunaga at Gokurakujiyama and then on Takamatsuyama in Ieyasu's camp. The move to Chausuyama is in nsenshi only (信長其營ヲ茶磨山ニ進メ). Remove [shincho]. Also mark "Danjōyama = Takamatsuyama" as a local identification.

15. **Low. Phase 1: Ōkubo "outside the palisade … as bait [nsenshi]".** nsenshi says Sakuma was bait (誘致) and Ōkubo was put out for flank attacks (側擊ニ充テ). Reword.

16. **Low. Unit `mochizuki`: "Mochizuki was killed".** The Mochizuki killed was Mochizuki Nobunaga (望月信永), Takeda Nobushige's son and Katsuyori's cousin (wpja l.75, l.191), not Nobumasa (信雅) as named in the unit. Name him.

17. **Low. Phase 9 text: "including … Naitō, Hara … both Sanada brothers [shincho]".** Shinchō's list of heads does not include Naitō, Hara or Sanada Masateru. Those come from Kōyō and nsenshi. Fix the citations.

18. **Low. Names.** Modern scholarship and Kofu city use Naitō **Masahide** (昌秀); Masatoyo is the Kōyō form (https://www.city.kofu.yamanashi.jp/welcome/rekishi/24generals/naito-masahide.html). Give both. Kofu city also gives Baba's birth as 1514(?).

19. **Low. Geography:**
    - The Rengo-gawa polyline stops at 34.927 N. The palisade's north end (34.9289) and the Sanada, Hashiba and Shibata action (34.928–34.931) have no stream drawn.
    - The second palisade on the Ōmiya-gawa behind Danjōyama (OSM 一重柵建設地, 34.9159, 137.5191) is not shown.
    - "Sakai's night ford" (34.9054, 137.5201) and the place of Naitō's death "near Miyawaki" (34.9233, 137.5303) have no source.

20. **Low. Unit `shibata`.** Shibata Katsuie's presence and flank attack rest on nsenshi alone. Shinchō's Nagashino chapter names only Takigawa, Hashiba and Niwa on Arumihara. Lower to low confidence and say so.

## Verified

1. **The reading of 出沢 is Suzawa, not Idesawa.** Japan Post postcode list for Shinshiro (出沢 スザワ 441-1313): https://www.post.japanpost.jp/cgi-zip/zipcode.php?pref=23&city=1232210. The same list confirms Arumi (有海), Ōmi (大海 オオミ), Takehiro, Sunaga and Norimoto.
2. **Matsudaira Koretada was killed at Nagashino.** Nihon Daihyakka Zensho, entry on Matsudaira Ietada: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%BE%E5%B9%B3%E5%AE%B6%E5%BF%A0-136638.
3. **Palisade and terrain.** The palisade stood along the Rengo-gawa at the foot of the middle-terrace scarp. The Takeda were on the high terrace to the east and attacked down the scarp into muddy alluvial ground. Aichi Prefecture, Shinshiro-Shitara geology page: https://www.pref.aichi.jp/shinshiroshitara/nature/geo/data/1-10_landform.html. This matches the map's palisade line and the Takeda start lines.
4. **Danjōyama.** Ieyasu's camp on Danjōyama was about 300 m from the Rengo-gawa, and there was a second palisade on the Ōmiya-gawa (tabi-mag, https://tabi-mag.jp/ai0251/). The map's distance is about 340 m.
5. **Saegusa Masasada died at Tobigasu**, with the castle-watch force, in a pincer attack. Kofu city: https://www.city.kofu.yamanashi.jp/welcome/rekishi/24generals/saigusa-masasada.html.
6. **Baba covered Katsuyori's escape as rearguard and died there.** He was never wounded before, and the Oda praised him as 比類なし (born 1514?). Kofu city: https://www.city.kofu.yamanashi.jp/welcome/rekishi/24generals/baba-nobuharu.html. This supports the unit note and phase 9.
7. **Baba's death site is on the west bank of the Toyokawa (Kansa) at Suzawa; his grave near Nagashino is a head-mound.** Supported by the 1740 *Mikawa no kuni futabamatsu* (於出沢之沢尻討死), as summarised in tabi-mag ai0572 and ai0571. Those two pages blocked direct fetching, so this rests on the search-result summaries. The map's death site is about 100 m west of the river.
8. **Kaneko Taku, *Nagashino kassen: teppōsen no kyozō to jitsuzō*** (Chūkō shinsho 2785, 2023, ISBN 978-4-12-102785-6) exists. It has a chapter on Sakai Tadatsugu and the Tobigasu attack, and on the screens as later constructions. Univ. of Tokyo Biblioplaza: https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/biblioplaza/ja/K_00154.html.
9. **Checked against the author's own primary texts:**
   - Shinchō's five waves, 1,000 guns under Sassa, Maeda, Nonomura, Fukutomi and Ban, Sakai at 戌刻, 辰刻, fighting to 未刻, about 10,000 dead, and Baba 無比類 are all accurate.
   - Kōyō's Baba 700 against Sakuma 6,000, about 80 left, unwounded, named himself, did not draw, aged 62, Kasai Higo's horse, and Yamagata's head carried home by Shimura are accurate.
   - nsenshi's Sassa urging Nobunaga out, Naitō dying near Miyawaki before Baba, Saruhashi, and the battle lasting 05:00–15:00 are accurate.
