# Nagashino (Shitaragahara), 29 June 1575 (Tenshō 3, 5th month, 21st day): research brief

**Why this battle.** Nathan asked for a big battle that includes the Baba family. Baba Mino-no-kami Nobuharu (also Nobufusa, 1514/15-1575), a Takeda senior retainer for forty years and later counted among Shingen's "four generals", fought his last battle here. He held the Takeda right wing and took the Maruyama knoll. He then covered Katsuyori's retreat and was killed. Ōta Gyūichi, Nobunaga's own chronicler, singled him out: "Baba Mino-no-kami's conduct was beyond compare" [shincho]. The Baba contingent and Baba's own movements are drawn as a featured thread through every phase.

**Dates.** Tenshō 3.5.21 = 29 June 1575 in the Julian calendar then used in Europe = 9 July 1575 proleptic Gregorian [wpja]. The sun is computed for 9 July (declination +22.4°).

## Overview

| | Oda Nobunaga and Tokugawa Ieyasu | Takeda Katsuyori |
|---|---|---|

| Detached | Sakai Tadatsugu's raid on Tobigasu: ~4,000 incl. 500 Oda guns [shincho] (~3,000 in [nsenshi]); Nagashino garrison ~500 under Okudaira Sadamasa [wpen] | — |
| Killed | ~6,000 killed and wounded (1903 estimate) [nsenshi]; ~300 at Tobigasu, including Matsudaira Koretada [nsenshi] [wpja] | ~10,000 "and countless drowned or starved" [shincho]; "over a thousand of the Kai *kunishū*" (*Tamon'in nikki*) and "several thousand horsemen" (*Kanemi-kyō ki*) [wpja]; ~370 heads at Tobigasu [nsenshi] |

The map shows 17 Takeda and 19 allied units summing to 15,000 and about 40,500 (Oda 31,000 incl. the guns with Sakai; Tokugawa 9,500 incl. Sakai's column and the garrison). Unit strengths inside the three Takeda wings and in the allied army are my division of the sourced totals, except where a source gives a figure (Baba 700, Naitō ~1,000, Yamagata 1,500, Sakuma ~6,000, Takigawa ~3,000 [koyo]; 1,000 guns [shincho]); every estimate is marked in the unit note.

## Phases

Times are local solar time. Sunrise on 9 July at 34.9° N is about 04:50.

**0 — day 1 (20th), 20:00: The night raid sets out.** The Takeda have crossed the Takisawa (Kansa) river and stand east of the Rengo-gawa: the right wing (Anayama, Baba, the Sanada brothers, Tsuchiya, Ichijō, ~3,000) near Asagi, 0.8 km NE of Maruyama; the centre (Takeda Nobukado, Naitō, Hara, Annaka, the West Kōzuke men, ~3,000) near Kiyoida; the left (Takeda Nobutoyo, Yamagata, Obata, Oyamada, Atobe, Amari, Ogasawara, ~3,000) on the heights south of Kiyoida; Katsuyori's reserve (~3,000) west of Arumi; 2,000 watch the castle and 1,000 hold the Tobigasu forts across the river [nsenshi] [koyo]. The allies are behind a palisade on the west bank of the Rengo-gawa, about 2 km away [shincho] [sites]. At 20:00 Sakai Tadatsugu's ~4,000 cross the Norimoto (Toyokawa) river in rain to circle south of the Takeda [shincho] [nsenshi]. Confidence: medium.

**1 — day 2 (21st), 04:45: Dawn — Tobigasu falls.** At first light Sakai storms the Tobigasu forts from behind; Kawakubo Nobuzane, Saegusa Masasada and others die; the garrison sorties to join him; Matsudaira Koretada of Sakai's column is killed by a counter-attack [nsenshi] [wpja] [shincho]. Hearing the war cries and seeing the smoke, Katsuyori orders the attack [nsenshi]. The Takeda advance to attack positions 300-600 m east of the Rengo-gawa, facing west [shincho] [nsenshi]. Sakuma Nobumori (with Ikeda and Mizuno) stands outside the palisade on and around the Maruyama knoll in the north as bait; the Ōkubo brothers stand outside it in the south, placed to strike the flank [nsenshi] [sites]. Nobunaga has moved to Chausuyama during the night (1903 history); Ieyasu is on Takamatsuyama, identified locally with Danjōyama [nsenshi] [shincho] [sites]. Confidence: medium.

**2 — 05:30: Yamagata's attack.** The first wave, Yamagata Masakage, drums beating, attacks in the south; the guns drive it back [shincho]. The 1903 history has Yamagata try to turn the allied right south of the Rengo bridge, find the stream too deep, and fight the Ōkubo brothers back and forth; *Kōyō gunkan* gives nine clashes [nsenshi] [koyo]. Confidence: medium.

**3 — 06:30: Baba takes Maruyama.** On the Takeda right Baba (700) drives Sakuma's men inside the palisade [koyo]; in the 1903 history he takes the Maruyama knoll and halts there [nsenshi]. In the centre Takeda Nobukado ("Shōyōken"), the second wave, attacks and is driven back by the guns [shincho]. Confidence: low (the Maruyama detail rests on the 1903 history; order disputed, see Disagreements 2).

**4 — 08:00: The third and fourth waves.** The Obata of West Kōzuke (in red, attacking mounted) and Takeda Nobutoyo's men attack in turn and are shot down; the allies stay behind the palisade [shincho]. Confidence: medium (hours not recorded).

**5 — 09:30: The Sanada and Tsuchiya at the palisade.** Baba holds Maruyama and sends the Sanada brothers and Tsuchiya Masatsugu forward; they break one palisade line but are shot down and flanked by Shibata Katsuie and Hashiba Hideyoshi coming round from the north; Sanada Nobutsuna, his brother Masateru and Tsuchiya are killed [nsenshi] [koyo] [sites]. Confidence: medium.

**6 — 11:00: Baba's own attack; Yamagata falls.** Shinchō's fifth wave is Baba himself, attacking with drums and losing many men like the others [shincho]. In the south Yamagata Masakage is shot through the body and killed [koyo] [nsenshi] [sites]. Confidence: medium (hour low).

**7 — 13:00: The Takeda break.** With the attacking wings shredded, Atobe's men run first; Anayama withdraws without having fought; Nobunaga, urged by Sassa, sends the whole army out of the palisade [nsenshi] [koyo]. Confidence: medium.

**8 — 14:00: The retreat and the rearguard.** The Takeda flee north towards Hōraiji, many drowning or falling at the river crossings [shincho]. Baba urges Katsuyori to go and covers him; Naitō Masatoyo dies near Miyawaki [nsenshi] [koyo]. Kasai Higo gives Katsuyori his own horse and is killed [koyo] [sites]. Confidence: medium.

**9 — 15:00: Baba's last stand.** Once Katsuyori's standard is out of sight, Baba turns to face the pursuit on a knoll at Suzawa above the Kansa river. He calls out his name and is killed, aged about 61-62; *Kōyō gunkan* says he did not draw his sword [nsenshi] [koyo] [sites]. The 1903 history ends the fighting at about 15:00 [nsenshi]. Confidence: medium (place disputed, see Disagreements 3).

## Disagreements and the readings chosen

1. **Strengths.** Traditional 38,000 against 15,000 (from *Shinchō kōki* and *Kōyō gunkan*) against Takayanagi's 16-18,000 against 8-10,000 [wpja]. *Chosen:* the traditional figures, which most modern accounts still use. The ratio (about 2.5:1) is similar under both, and the narrow field limits how many fought at once [wpja].
2. **When Baba attacked.** In *Shinchō kōki* Baba is the *fifth* and last wave [shincho]. *Kōyō gunkan* lists his success against Sakuma first among the places the Takeda won, but gives no sequence [koyo]. The capture of and halt on Maruyama come from the 1903 history alone [nsenshi]. *Chosen:* both, in sequence. Baba takes Maruyama early (phase 3, after the Takeda tradition) and makes a final attack in phase 6 (Shinchō's fifth wave). The primary source's order is kept for the waves, and this reconciliation is stated as mine.
3. **Where Baba died.** The 1903 history and the Shinshiro marker put it on a knoll at Suzawa (出沢), about 2.5 km north of the castle on the Kansa river [nsenshi] [sites]. *Kōyō gunkan* puts it at "the Nagashino bridge site" (長篠橋場) [koyo], and his grave stands just west of the castle [sites]. *Chosen:* Suzawa, where two sources agree; the bridge-site tradition is in the debate field. The grave is shown as a place.
4. **How he died.** *Kōyō gunkan*: unwounded until then, he named himself and was speared by four or five men without drawing his sword; ja.wikipedia (after later accounts): he offered his head to Kawai Sanjūrō, a man of Ban Naomasa [koyo] [wpja]. *Chosen:* given as "killed", with both versions in the note.
5. **Guns and the "three-rank volley".** *Shinchō kōki*: about 1,000 guns under five commanders, plus 500 with Sakai [shincho]. This counts only those detachments; contingents' own guns, the Tokugawa guns and guns levied from other lords are not counted, so the army's total is unknown and probably well over 1,500 [wpja] [wpen]. Later Edo works and the 1903 history: 3,000 guns firing 1,000 at a time [nsenshi] [wpen]. *Chosen:* the 1,000-gun detachment as a unit, total left unknown; the alternating volley is treated as a myth (below).
7. **When Tobigasu fell.** The 1903 history and ja.wikipedia: at first light, as the trigger for the Takeda attack; *Shinchō kōki*: Sakai's banners went up at the hour of the dragon (07:00-09:00) [nsenshi] [wpja] [shincho]. *Chosen:* dawn (phase 1).
8. **Where the Takeda generals died.** *Kōyō gunkan* and the 1903 history kill the Sanada brothers and Tsuchiya at the palisade; recent research summarised in en.wikipedia holds that most senior deaths came in the retreat [koyo] [nsenshi] [wpen]. *Chosen:* the traditional places, with the newer view stated in phase 8.
9. **The castle watch.** The 1903 history: it withdrew only after the main defeat, pursued by the garrison (Kōsaka Masazumi killed); ja.wikipedia: Sakai's column swept it from Arumi [nsenshi] [wpja]. *Chosen:* the 1903 sequence.
6. **Takeda losses.** ~10,000 [shincho] against "over a thousand Kai *kunishū*" [wpja]. *Chosen:* ~10,000 in the forces row with the range in the note; the chart's per-moment losses are estimates.

## Myths

1. **The three-rank rotating volley of 3,000 guns**: first in Oze Hoan's *Shinchōki* (1622) and the 1903 staff history, not in *Shinchō kōki*; questioned since Fujimoto Masayuki (1980) [wpen] [wpja].
2. **A Takeda "cavalry charge" mown down by guns**: modern scholars (Suzuki Masaya and others) argue the Takeda fought largely on foot like everyone else [wpja]. Whether they were outgunned is disputed: the Takeda had used guns for years, but analysis of shot from the field suggests they were short of lead and powder, while Nobunaga controlled the trade ports [wpen] [wpja]. *Shinchō kōki* does describe the West Kōzuke men as good horsemen who attacked mounted [shincho]. The map shows contingents as mixed infantry.
3. **A reckless Katsuyori overriding wise elders** (the council scene): from *Kōyō gunkan* and later works; ja.wikipedia notes this is not in reliable sources [wpja] [koyo].
