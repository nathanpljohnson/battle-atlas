# Agincourt, 25 October 1415 (Julian; 3 November Gregorian): research brief

## Overview
Henry V's army was retreating from Harfleur to Calais, sick and short of food. It found the French blocking the road between the woods of Azincourt and Tramecourt. After a stand-off of several hours, Henry advanced to within long bowshot and re-planted his archers' stakes. That provoked the French to attack: first a cavalry charge that broke on the stakes, then a dismounted vanguard and main battle that toiled through deep mud under arrow fire, jammed into the narrowing field and were destroyed in the press. A raid on the English baggage, and the sight of the French rearguard forming, led Henry to order most of the prisoners killed. The rearguard then left the field.

## Phases (approximate local time; sunrise ~07:15)
0. **07:30 Dawn.** English in three "battles" (Camoys on the left/west, Henry in the centre, York on the right/east), four deep, with archers on the flanks, about 1.0 km south of the Azincourt–Tramecourt road and 0.7 km north of Maisoncelle, facing north [wp][nic]. French vanguard of dismounted men-at-arms across the field about 0.1 km south of the road, with wings either side, cavalry "horns" on both flanks, the main battle a bowshot behind, missile troops behind them, and the mounted rearguard at the back, facing south [wp][nic]. Confidence: medium (the site itself is debated).
1. **10:30 Henry advances.** After three to four hours of waiting (the French stood "until nine or ten" [nic]), the English advance to within long bowshot, about 250–300 m, and re-plant their stakes [wp][nic]. Medium.
2. **11:00 The cavalry charge.** Arrows provoke the French; the under-strength cavalry wings charge the archers, fail against the stakes and the woods, and bolt back through the advancing vanguard [wp][nic]. Medium.
3. **11:15 The vanguard advances.** The men-at-arms struggle ~250 m through ploughed mud under arrow fire and converge on the three English banners, packing the narrowing front [wp]. Medium.
4. **11:30 The press.** The English line is pushed back, then holds. Archers come out from the stakes with hatchets and mallets against the flanks of the jammed mass, and heaps of dead form at the three banners [wp]. Medium.
5. **12:30 The main battle is swallowed.** The second line joins and is engulfed. Alençon is killed near Henry; Brabant arrives late and is killed [wp]. Low–medium.
6. **13:30 Alarm in the rear.** Ysembart d'Azincourt's raid on the baggage at Maisoncelle, and the rearguard forming, lead Henry to order the prisoners killed [wp]. The timing of the raid is disputed (below). Low.
7. **14:30 The rearguard leaves.** The French rearguard does not attack and withdraws; the English hold the field [wp]. Medium.

## Disagreements and choices
- **Numbers.** Curry (records; 8,700 against 12,000) versus Barker, Sumption and Rogers (chroniclers; about 6,000 against 14,000–24,000). **Chosen:** English about 8,000, because Curry's pay-record method is strongest for the English side, and Mortimer accepts 8,000–9,000 as plausible. French 14,500 fighting men, because Rogers, Mortimer and Sumption converge on about 10,000 men-at-arms (after the Berry herald, an eyewitness) plus 4,000–5,000 missile troops. The valets are shown separately as the rearguard [wp].
- **The site.** The traditional field lies between the Azincourt and Tramecourt woods. Sutherland (2015) and Livingston (2019) argue for a site west of Azincourt, partly because archaeology has found nothing at the traditional site. **Chosen:** the traditional site, as in every map consulted except Hardy's, with medium/low confidence on all coordinates [wp].
- **The archers' deployment.** Archers on the flanks only, or also in wedges between the battles (the "herse" debate). **Chosen:** flanks plus a small low-confidence group in the centre [wp].
- **The baggage raid.** At the start (Barker, after the Gesta) or near the end (Le Fèvre, Monstrelet). **Chosen:** shown near the end, where most chronicles place it and where it explains the prisoner killing, with the alternative recorded [wp].

## Myths
- **"The V sign comes from archers whose fingers the French would cut off."** Unsupported. Burgundian chroniclers report the threat [wp], but the gesture's origin story is modern folklore. Omitted.
- **"Arrows killed most of the French."** Modern scholarship stresses the crush, the mud and close combat; the arrows mainly disordered and funnelled the attack [wp]. The narrative says so.
- **The hidden archers in the Tramecourt meadow.** Reported by some French writers; Le Fèvre, who was with the English army, says it was untrue [nic]. Not shown.

## Unit confidence
- Medium: the three English battles and the flank archers; the French vanguard and main battle. All coordinates are low, because of the site debate.
- Low: the central archers, the French wings, the cavalry horns' exact places, the missile troops, the rearguard size, Brabant, and the baggage raiders.
