# Independent review (general-purpose reviewer agent, 2026-10-16)

## Verdict

The reconstruction has the broad sequence right: the stand-off, the English advance, the failed cavalry charge, the press at the three banners, the baggage raid, the killing of the prisoners. Its handling of the numbers and the site debates is fair. But several things that a reader of the map would take away are wrong or not supported by the sources. The last phase says the French rearguard "never attacks", yet Monstrelet describes a final charge by Marle and Fauquembergue, and so does the author's own French Wikipedia source. The French wings are on the wrong sides, because "left" was read from the English point of view. The cavalry charge is drawn with about six times the riders Monstrelet gives. The English line is about twice the 750 yards the author's own source gives, and both armies' flanks run 100–200 m into the drawn woods. The grave-pit calvary and the French line sit 300–600 m south of where the georeferenced 1899 map (which the method says it follows) and OpenStreetMap put them. The order of battle comes almost entirely from Wikipedia and is presented as if observed, when it is partly a modern reconstruction that the chroniclers contradict. None of this needs new research, only corrections, but phases 2 and 7 and the French deployment should not be published as they stand. Note that validation.md reports 0 errors, but the validator plainly does not check units against the woods.

## Issues

1. **High: phase 7 (14:30), unit `rearguard`, narrative "The French rearguard never attacks; it melts away".** Contradicted by Monstrelet: "The count de Marle, the count de Fauquemberg, the lords de Louvroy and du Chin, had with some difficulty retained about six hundred men-at-arms, with whom they made a gallant charge on the English … they were all killed or made prisoners." The same passage is in Nicolas's translation (nicolas1833.txt, around line 12362). The author's own `frwp` source says the third line "charge et se brise sur les Anglais" (frwp_azincourt.wiki line 94). It also explains why Marle and Fauquembergue are among the nine dead counts the narrative lists. Evidence: Monstrelet, *Chronicles*, tr. T. Johnes, ch. CXLVII, https://deremilitari.org/RESOURCES/SOURCES/agincourt.htm. **Fix:** in phase 6 or 7, add a charge of about 600 men-at-arms out of the rearguard under Marle and Fauquembergue, destroyed in front of the English line. Rewrite the phase 7 text to "most of the rearguard fled; a remnant charged and was destroyed". Drop phase 7's confidence to low.

2. **High: units `wing_w` "Left wing (Count of Vendôme)" placed west, and `wing_e` "Right wing (Richemont)" placed east.** The French face south, so the French left is the **east** (Tramecourt) side. Monstrelet says Vendôme's wing was "to fall on the right flank of the English", which is also the east. The mounted wing under Clignet de Brabant was "to attack the left flank", the west. Wikipedia's "the left under the Count of Vendôme" is consistent with this. The model has swapped the two. Separately, splitting the cavalry into a Clignet "left horn" and a Bosredon "right horn" is invented. Monstrelet and Le Fèvre (nicolas1833.txt lines 11970 and 12225) put Clignet and Louis de Bosredon jointly at the head of the same 800 picked horsemen. Evidence: Monstrelet, ch. CXLVI, deremilitari URL above. **Fix:** swap the two wings. Label the cavalry horns "Clignet de Brabant and Louis de Bosredon (jointly)", or leave them unnamed. Keep both flanks only with a note that the chroniclers describe one mounted wing aimed at the English left.

3. **High: geometry of `archers_l`, `archers_r`, `wing_w`, `wing_e`, `cav_w`, `cav_e` against `B.woods`.** I computed the gap between the drawn wood ellipses: about 890 m at E1 and about 1,060 m at F0. The English array spans about 1,290 m. In phases 1–3 the archers' outer ends run 187 m (west) and 210 m (east) into the woods. The French wings run up to 150 m into the woods and the horns up to 129 m. The source the author relies on says Henry deployed "across a 750 yd (690 m) part of the defile" (wp_clean.txt line 77). The phase 2 text says "the woods prevent any outflanking", but the map shows the archers standing inside them. **Fix:** compress the English array to about 700–900 m, with the archers anchored *on* the wood edges. Shorten the archer frontages (2,900 archers several deep, behind stakes, do not need 330 m each). Pull the French wings and horns inside the gap. Add a woods-overlap check to the validator.

4. **Medium: units `cav_w`/`cav_e`; phase 2 men 400+400; the "~500 mounted men-at-arms" labels.** The model shows 1,000 horsemen and 800 charging. Monstrelet: the 800 "were reduced to seven score, who vainly attempted it". Guillaume de Saveuse led them and was shot dead. The narrative does say "short of men", but the numbers on the map do not reflect it. Evidence: Monstrelet, ch. CXLVI (deremilitari); also nicolas1833.txt around line 12305. Wikipedia's 800–1,200 is the *planned* strength. **Fix:** show the planned strength at dawn and roughly 150–420 charging. Add Saveuse to the note. Mark the strength confidence low.

5. **Medium: `B.hill("Calvaire (French grave pits)", (50.4618, 2.1405))` and F0 = 50.4632.** OSM puts the "Calvaire de la Gacogne", the wayside cross on the Route de Blangy, at **50.4652, 2.1406**. That is north of the Azincourt–Tramecourt road and about 380 m north of where the model puts it. Using georef.json, I converted the 1899 map's arrows to coordinates: the French arrows fall at about 50.4657–50.4696 (well north of the road), and its "Cemetery" at about 50.4661, which agrees with OSM. The English arrows fall at about 50.4588. The model's French vanguard sits on the road, and brief.md says "0.1 km south of the road" although the code puts it about 20 m south. Both conflict with the map the method claims to follow. Evidence: https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=calvaire+azincourt&format=json; sources/fbb1899.jpg with georef.json. **Fix:** move the calvary to OSM's position. Either move the French dawn line north of the road, or state that the model departs from the 1899 map and say why. Bring the brief and the code into agreement.

6. **Medium: phase 0 narrative, "The chroniclers put the gap between the armies at anything from 250 paces to a quarter of a mile [nic]".** Nicolas's footnote (nicolas1833.txt, around line 5503) is about the armies' night positions. It also gives Fenin's "four bow-shots" and Monstrelet's "three bow-shots", which the narrative leaves out. The figures the narrative keeps (about 230–400 m) contradict the 1.02 km dawn gap on the map (F0 − E0 = 0.0092°). **Fix:** quote the full range ("a quarter-mile to four bowshots, between the camps on the night before"), and say the dawn gap shown, about 1 km, is a modern estimate.

7. **Medium: the French order of battle (all `van_*`, `wing_*`, `main_*`, `cav_*`), and brief.md "French order of battle [wp]".** The figures 4,800 / 3,000 / 600 / 600 / 800–1,200 / 200 are a modern reconstruction. They appear to draw on the Somme battle plan of mid-October, BL Cotton Caligula D V, published by Phillpotts in 1984. The chroniclers give something else. Monstrelet and Le Fèvre have a vanguard of about 8,000 "helmets", Vendôme's wing at 1,500–1,600, and Richemont in the *vanguard*. That last point makes `van_e` "Eu, Richemont's men" and `wing_e` "Richemont" a double-count. The vanguard note also says "some 16 ranks deep", but 4,800 men on 3×330 m of frontage is about 5 deep. Evidence: Monstrelet, ch. CXLVI (deremilitari); Phillpotts 1984 (below); BL image record https://imagesonline.bl.uk/asset/5181/. **Fix:** in the brief, add a disagreement entry, "plan vs. chronicle order of battle". Make the commander attributions match one source. Narrow the frontages to the claimed depth, or drop "16 ranks".

8. **Medium: brief.md losses row, and `strengthNote` "Monstrelet's 600".** Monstrelet, in both Johnes's translation and Nicolas's, gives English dead as "about sixteen hundred men of all ranks". The 600 is Hardy's figure, via frwp. The "~400 illustrative midpoint" has no source; English sources give dead in double figures, and the record evidence is at least 112. **Fix:** cite "Monstrelet/Waurin 1,600". Show English dead as a range (about 112 to several hundred) and drop the invented midpoint.

9. **Medium: unit `rearguard`, labelled "Rearguard (Dammartin, Fauquembergue)" but described as "A leaderless mounted reserve".** The label and the note contradict each other. Monstrelet and Le Fèvre name Marle, Dammartin, Fauquembergue and Louvroy as its leaders (nicolas1833.txt line 11979; deremilitari). Marle is missing. The ~5,000 strength is an unsourced figure: Rogers counts about 10,000 *gros valets* for the whole army, and Mortimer says they were non-combatant pages. **Fix:** name Marle. Change "leaderless" to "with few of its leaders left after the lords went forward". Show a 5,000–10,000 range, or say the figure is a guess.

10. **Medium: phases 5–7, units `cav_w`/`cav_e`, `van_*`, `main_*`, `wing_*`.** Routed or annihilated French units stay on the field as formed lines until the end. The cavalry (350 each) stand at F0+0.003 for 3.5 hours after they "fled". The main battle still has 600 men in line at E1+0.0024 in phase 7. That contradicts the narrative ("engulfed", killed, captured or fled). **Fix:** remove or rout these units after phase 3 (cavalry) and phase 5 or 6 (the dismounted lines). Show the survivors as prisoners or fugitives.

11. **Low: `raiders` at dawn (50.4560, 2.1180), and `brabant` at dawn (50.4800, 2.1450).** Placing a deployed 650-strong raiding force at dawn implies a planned operation, which is one side of a debate the note itself says is open. Brabant was still marching to the field that morning. **Fix:** make both appear only from phase 4 or 5.

12. **Low: `meta.wind = 225` (wind from the south-west, behind the English archers).** No source is given for the wind. If the viewer renders it, it is invented detail that favours the English. **Fix:** remove it, or cite a source.

13. **Low: phase 3, "men in the third rank can hardly lift their swords".** The "third rank" detail has no source. Monstrelet says only that everyone except the front line "could not raise their hands". Likewise the phase 0 tactic box, "Period manuals held that the side which advances … loses", has no citation. **Fix:** follow Monstrelet's wording and cite the tactic claim, or soften it.

14. **Low: brief.md and phase 6 debate, "Le Fèvre and Monstrelet put it near the end".** Monstrelet places the news of the raid "during the heat of the combat", and the Gesta ties the prisoner order to the rearguard re-forming. The timing chosen is defensible, but the statement overstates what the sources say. **Fix:** "during the later fighting".

15. **Low: brief.md "Sutherland (2015) and Livingston (2019)".** Sutherland's argument is in a 2005 article (below). Livingston's full case is his 2023 book; in 2019 he had only a magazine piece. **Fix:** cite the originals.

16. **Low (confidence labels).** Phase 7 is labelled medium; it should be low (see issue 1). Phase 2 is labelled medium, but the charge strength should be low. Phase 0 is labelled medium while the woods and line geometry are demonstrably off. Either lower it to low, or fix issues 3 and 5 first.

## Verified

- **The cavalry force was "reduced to seven score", and Saveuse was shot dead.** Monstrelet, tr. Johnes, at deremilitari.org (URL above).
- **The Marle–Fauquembergue charge of about 600 men-at-arms, all killed or taken.** Same source.
- **Vendôme's wing was aimed at the English right; Clignet and Bosredon's 800 at the English left; the vanguard was about 8,000 and included Richemont.** Same source.
- **Monstrelet gives English dead as about 1,600, and the raiders as about 600 peasants with Bournonville, Clamasse and Ysembart d'Azincourt.** Same source. The raiders' strength in the model is correct.
- **Maisoncelles was "about three bow-shots" from the French camp.** Same source. It supports showing a gap of several hundred metres to 1 km before the advance.
- **The chronology is broadly right.** An advance about 10:00 and fighting from about 11:00 match the Historical Association / Agincourt 600 resource pack (https://www.history.org.uk/files/download/17052/1453921932/HAAgincourt_600_History_resource_pack.pdf) and Nicolas's "11 a.m." marginal note. Le Fèvre's "until nine or ten" checks out in Nicolas's translation. The same school pack states the hidden archers at Tramecourt as fact, which shows that myth is still in circulation. The brief is right to flag it.
- **Sunrise.** I recomputed it for 50.46°N with declination −15.2°: about 07:12–07:17 apparent solar time, so "~07:15" holds.
- **Sutherland's survey found almost no artefacts at the traditional site.** Medievalists.net summary of *Journal of Conflict Archaeology* 1 (2005): https://www.medievalists.net/2013/09/the-battle-of-agincourt-an-alternative-location
- **The calvary's position.** OSM / Nominatim gives "Calvaire de la Gacogne" at 50.4652, 2.1406.
