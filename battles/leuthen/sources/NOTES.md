# Leuthen, 5 December 1757: source notes

## Official history (primary framework)
- `gs1904_search.txt` (+ `gs1904_pageindex.json`, `gs1904_djvu.txt`): Großer Generalstab, *Die Kriege Friedrichs des Großen*, III. Teil: *Der Siebenjährige Krieg 1756–1763*, Bd. 6: *Leuthen* (Berlin: Mittler, 1904), archive.org `diekriegefriedrit3b6prus`. Built on the Prussian war archive (Gaudi's journal, regimental journals, the king's relations) and Austrian, Bavarian, Württemberg, Saxon and French archival reports (Vienna Kriegsarchiv, Munich, Stuttgart, Dresden, Paris). The OCR misreads Fraktur (ſ→f, d→b, h→§ etc.) but is legible; page indexes below are hOCR page indexes. Translated key points (German → English):
  - p. 17 (idx 31): On 4 Dec the Austrians camped in two lines east of Nippern, Frobelwitz and Leuthen, Nádasdy and the Reserve as a third line behind. Charles and Daun decided to accept battle and ordered readiness an hour before dawn, "but only to occupy a defensive position". Strength: **65,000 men in 85 battalions and 125 squadrons, about 5,000 of them light troops; 170 battalion guns and 65 heavy guns** (Anhang 7). Württemberg report: Nádasdy's corps arrived 7–8 pm, the Württembergers only at 11 pm.
  - p. 18: Prussian orders: avant-garde under arms at 04:30, main body at 04:00, no drums.
  - p. 19–20 (idx 33–34): **Terrain**: low, flat-domed rises; noteworthy are the Breslauer Berg east of Frobelwitz, the Galgenberg and Lüttwitz height north of Borne, the ridge south of Borne formed by the **Schleier-, Butter-, Sophien-, Heide- and Wachberg**, and the Sagschütz hill group, of which the **Kiefernberg** south of Sagschütz was then wooded and visible from afar (now ploughland). Woods north of the old road. The Briegswasser at the Zettelfurt flows through a bog several hundred metres wide; bog west and north-west of Nippern; ditched meadows south of the Leuthen and Rathen woods (Busch). The Weistritz (Schweidnitzer Wasser) meadow valley crossable only by dams and bridges. **Most of the ponds then present have since been drained**; some may have been fished out and drained that autumn, and the frost may have made the wet meadows south of Sagschütz passable. **Weather**: snow on 3–4 Dec; the night of 4–5 mild and clear; ground frozen hard with a thin snow cover; **dense fog in the morning of the 5th, lifting towards midday to clear weather**; snow again after dark.
  - p. 20–21: Prussian march: avant-garde = all hussars, 3 free battalions, foot Jäger, Württemberg Dragoons, 9 battalions. At 06:00 the main body (infantry in 2 columns, cavalry outer columns) joined between Neumarkt and Kammendorf. The 10 Glogau fortress 12-pounders ("Brummer") marched in front of the main-body infantry.
  - p. 21–22 (idx 35–36): **Borne**: in the morning fog a line of horse west of Borne: Nostitz's Saxon chevau-legers and Austrian hussars (Morocz), Croats in the Borne and Lampersdorf woods. 30 squadrons of the Prussian first line charged; the Saxons broke, 11 officers and ~600 men taken with 2 standards; hussars pursued past Gross-Heidau to the Austrian front. Anhang 8: contact not before 08:00 (full daylight).
  - p. 22–23 (idx 36–37): On news of the Prussian advance Charles moved the whole army **1,000–2,000 paces forward**: right-wing cavalry against a small wood SE of Nippern, the infantry line **west** of Frobelwitz and Leuthen, hooked round Leuthen; heavy guns in four batteries before the front. Reserve corps behind the right wing; its grenadiers, pickets and Croats in the Zettelbusch. **Nádasdy to the heights at Sagschütz, flank formed facing SW, heavy guns in two batteries, abatis before the Kiefernberg**; Nostitz with 3 chevau-leger and 2 hussar regiments joined him. Anhang 10: the earlier Prussian plans (all from Gaudi) show the night camp line east of the villages, not the battle position; the Vienna plan by Captain v. Walter and Lt v. Wiercker (Kriegsarchiv) and Gaudi's own relation put the battle position west of them; Nádasdy's first line (Württembergers) stood on the west and south slopes of the Sagschütz hills, not behind them.
  - p. 23: Frederick rode through Borne (occupied by the 3 free battalions and Jäger) to the **Schönberg** with Prince Moritz and saw the whole Austrian line except the right-wing cavalry hidden by the Zettelbusch. Decided to attack Nádasdy, moving the army behind the heights south of Borne towards Kertschütz.
  - p. 23–24 (idx 37–38): **Feint**: the avant-garde cavalry formed ~1,000 paces east of Borne; the left columns passed north of Borne, the right through and south of it; once level east of Borne the king ordered deployment, carried out only in part, to make the enemy think he would attack the right wing north of Frobelwitz. **About 10:30.** Charles and Daun watched from the windmill height of the Breslauer Berg. Lucchesi reported repeatedly that his wing was threatened and begged for reinforcement; Montazet (French brigadier at HQ) objected ("only an army of snipes could manoeuvre among those marshes"). Charles sent the **Reserve corps to the right wing** (3 battalions in Nippern, 5 SE of it) and **Serbelloni with most of the left-wing cavalry** towards Guckerwitz, Daun going with him.
  - p. 24–25 (idx 38–39): **The march south**: the army wheeled from wing-wise into line-wise columns and turned south, direction point the **Zobtenberg**. The 6 avant-garde battalions under Prince Karl of Bevern stayed before the right-wing cavalry; Wedel's 3 battalions marched on the left at the head of the first infantry line. Zieten hussars scouted ahead; Puttkamer hussars to the left cavalry wing. Heavy guns in 5 batteries left of the first line behind Wedel; artillery and infantry **ammunition wagons between the lines** (first mention; Anhang 13: some battalions fired over 180 rounds). 25 hussar squadrons screened on the ridge east of the march, the king and Moritz riding with them. From the Frobelwitz heights the Austrians could see the turn south, but the **Schleier- and Sophienberg hid the rest** (Anhang 14: in a 1904 trial a large white flag carried along the route was never visible from the Breslauer Berg windmill). Daun: "Die guten Leute paschen ab; lassen wir sie" ("the good folk are slipping off; let them go").
  - p. 25–26 (idx 39–40): **Soon after 12:00** the column heads, having passed west of Lobetinz towards Schriegwitz, reached Schriegwitz. Zieten's right-wing cavalry through and round Schriegwitz with Bevern's 6 battalions as flank guard. Line formed by wheeling platoons left. **Driesen's left-wing cavalry north of the Heideberg facing east**, covered by the rises. **Wedel's 3 battalions as the "Attacke"** before the infantry's right. 25 hussar squadrons and the Württemberg Dragoons behind the second line's centre as reserve. Brummer battery left of the Attacke; two heavy batteries before the centre, one before each wing. Order: the Attacke half-right against the Sagschütz Kiefernberg, both infantry lines following **in battalion echelons 50 paces apart**. **About 13:00**; "barely four hours of daylight". The king on the **Wachberg south of Lobetinz**.
  - p. 27 (idx 41): Wedel's 3 battalions inclined sharply right; the Brummer battery fired from the start, then unlimbered on the **Glanzberg**. A wet ditch with willows 300 paces short of the Kiefernberg. 3 Württemberg battalions in the Kiefernbusch replied with guns and volleys; after one volley Wedel went in with the bayonet and was among the guns at the second enemy volley. The Württemberg regiments to the left and 3 grenadier battalions in the **Faulbusch** gave way without being attacked. 7 regimental guns taken.
  - p. 28 (idx 42): Bevern's 6 battalions on the extreme right flank: Nádasdy massed **43 squadrons** behind the Faulbusch and charged Zieten; the battalions' musketry and canister drove them off; **Nostitz fell, bleeding from fourteen wounds**, prisoner. Nádasdy rallied behind the **Gohlauer Berg**. The right battalion of the first line followed Wedel at 100 paces' depth, and the echelon developed. Wedel passed east of Sagschütz against the **Kirchberg**.
  - p. 28–29 (idx 42–43): North of the Kiefernberg Austrian regiments from Nádasdy's right and second line were pushed hastily in front of the Bavarians; three of them (Maguire, Johann Pálffy, Haller) gave way (Seyssel d'Aix). "This is the first and only time [the oblique order] was carried right through before the enemy." Prussian batteries of the right wing and two of the centre on the **Judenberg**. Wedel's 3 battalions and grenadier battalion Kremzow (led by Moritz) took the Austrian battery on the Kirchberg. Nádasdy rallied behind the deep **Gohlau ditch**; Moritz brought up Bevern's 6 battalions round its right; the 12-pdr battery followed to the Kirchberg, the 3 Judenberg batteries to its left.
  - p. 30–31 (idx 44–45): **Zieten led 53 squadrons between the Faulbusch and the Gohlau ponds**; ditches forced piecemeal charges; Lentulus checked by flank fire; Krockow wounded and captured; Lentulus took 15 guns and colours and nearly destroyed the **Jung-Modena Dragoons**; the Savoy Dragoons lost heavily; Seelen's Zieten Hussars took about 2,000 prisoners. Nádasdy's cavalry rallied north of the **Rathener Busch**; his infantry fled to the Rathen wood and behind the Weistritz. Zieten halted with the right-wing cavalry south of the brook that joins the Weistritz at Arnoldsmühle.
  - p. 31–32 (idx 45–46): Charles ordered the Reserve corps south through Leuthen and a **general left wheel with Leuthen as pivot**, south front of the village to be held; done in haste, partly in file, through fugitives. The reserve arrived at Leuthen first, in disorder; Arenberg tried to form a front south of Leuthen with a refused flank; Serbelloni's remaining squadrons covered it until cannon fire drove them behind the left wing at the Leuthener Busch.
  - p. 33 (idx 47): The Austrian battalions south of Leuthen fell back to the **heights north of the village**. The main mass of Austrian infantry was crammed into too narrow a space, "**in places 100 ranks deep**". Battalions of the left-wing second line held Leuthen and threw up a breastwork; all remaining artillery unlimbered on the **windmill height**. **More than 70 squadrons under Lucchesi and Serbelloni** assembled to the side and rear of the right wing, hidden by "the Schönberg and height 141". Ligne: "the third battalion of the royal Guard … fired at us at 80 paces".
  - p. 34–35 (idx 48–49): The king moved from the Wachberg to the Radaxdorf area, under heavy fire from the windmill height. **Driesen with 50 squadrons stayed west of the Sophienberg** to cover the left. **About 15:30 the infantry stormed the 1,800-pace front of Leuthen.** Battalion **Roth-Würzburg** held the walled Catholic churchyard; the Garde and grenadier battalion Retzow assaulted in vain until **III/Garde under Captain v. Möllendorff** forced the west gate; a breach was shot in the south wall. Roth-Würzburg: 4 officers and 33 men escaped with one of four colours. Village cleared after **about half an hour**. Second-line battalions filled gaps; the left wing came level and stood in a firefight west of the village with the refused Austrian right; some battalions wavered under fire from the windmill height; one battalion from the second line restored them. The king put the left-wing heavy battery on the **SE slope of the Butterberg** with the Brummer battery; centre and right batteries south of Leuthen. All infantry committed. Ammunition replenished.
  - p. 35–36 (idx 49–50): The line advanced N of Leuthen across a deep ditch along the road at the north edge of the village against dense, mostly unfought infantry (incl. the grenadier corps); the 9 battalions under Moritz outflanked again; but "the battle stood; the short day was ending".
  - p. 36–37 (idx 50–51): **Lucchesi**, seeing Prussian progress against the Austrian right NW of Leuthen, left his covered position **SE of Gross-Heidau** to strike between the Butterberg and Leuthen at the apparently open Prussian left; he did not reconnoitre south. **Driesen** watching from the Sophienberg wheeled left, moved north behind the rises until he overlapped, wheeled right and appeared on the heights in their flank. First line: **15 cuirassier squadrons and the 10 squadrons of the Bayreuth Dragoons**; second line 15 cuirassier squadrons; third, overlapping left, the **Puttkamer Hussars**. Bayreuth took the right flank, the hussars the rear; **30 squadrons of the reserve under Prince [Eugen] of Württemberg** joined; the Austrian horse fled into its own infantry. **Lucchesi killed** in the melee. Panic in the right-wing infantry; whole battalions threw down their muskets; all made for the Weistritz; centre and left followed as the Prussian infantry advanced again. An attempt to form a line between Frobelwitz and Lissa failed at once.
  - p. 37–38 (idx 51–52): Angelelli with the Jäger and 3 free battalions came up from Borne. Regiments **Wallis and Baden-Durlach** made a last stand on the windmill height, ridden over by the Carabiniers and Bayreuth Dragoons and attacked by free battalion Angelelli; grenadier battalion **Schenckendorff** took the batteries there. The flight poured **over Saara and Klein-Heidau to the Weistritz bridges**; Nádasdy rallied some battalions and held the crossings at Lissa and upstream; the Prussian pursuit stopped at the stream; heavy guns at Klein-Heidau fired across.
  - p. 38–39 (idx 52–53): Prussian infantry halted north of the Breslau road. **About 18:00**, in renewed snow and darkness, the king led grenadier battalions Manteuffel, Wedel and Ramin and the Seydlitz Cuirassiers to Lissa (the Saara innkeeper with a lantern beside his horse). Volleys at the entrance; shortly **before 19:00** a last fight for the two bridges east of Lissa; the king spent the night in the Lissa Schloss.
  - p. 40 (idx 54): "During the night advance on Lissa individual men struck up 'Nun danket alle Gott'; the hymn passed from regiment to regiment" (the GS presents this as fact; see Myths). The army camped between Lissa and Guckerwitz on snow.
  - p. 41 (idx 55): **Losses**. Prussian: 63 officers and 1,116 men killed, 164 officers and 5,043 men wounded; total 223 officers and 6,159 men [sic; the components give 227 + 6,159 = 6,386; Wikipedia gives 6,344]. Austrian, on the field alone: about 3,000 killed, 6,000–7,000 wounded and over 12,000 prisoners; **46 colours, 9 standards, 131 guns**. Dead: Lucchesi, the Saxon Nostitz (of wounds), Prince Stolberg, Ottenwolf. Anhang 20: Bavarians lost 49 officers 581 men; Württembergers 61 officers 2,189 men (mostly missing). Anhang 22: prisoners taken at Leuthen and in the pursuit (official list): 307 officers, 21,500 men.
  - Anhang 9 (Prussian strength): 48½ battalions, 133 squadrons; about **35,000 men** (Frederick wrote "39,000" to Prince Henry on 1 Dec and "35,000" to Wilhelmine on 8 Dec). Anhang 7: contemporaries put the Austrians at 60,000 (the king), 70,000 (Eichel, Gaudi, Cogniazzo), 85,000 (Seyssel), 90,000 (Tempelhof).
  - Anhang 5: the Parchwitz speech is genuine in substance, not in wording (Retzow's 1802 text is a later compilation). Anhang 15: the story that the Württembergers all turned at once crying "Long live the King of Prussia" is untrue for the Kiefernberg garrison (Roeder lost 8 officers and 211 men, 20%); the Württembergers fought reluctantly and other battalions had low bloody losses but very many missing. Anhang 18: the Lissa anecdote (the king surprised by Austrian officers in the Schloss, "Bon soir, Messieurs") is a legend (Droysen, Kutzen, Grünhagen, Koser); at most he said it to wounded officers.

### Prussian order of battle (Anlage 1)
- Avant-garde cavalry (G.L. Prince Eugen of Württemberg, G.M. v. Stechow): Puttkamer Hus. 10 sqns, II/Szekely Hus. 5, Württemberg Drag. 5, Seydlitz Hus. 4, Warnery Hus. 6, Werner Hus. 10, Zieten Hus. 10 = 50 sqns. Avant-garde infantry (G.M. Prince Karl of Bevern): Fußjäger, free battalions Angelelli, Kalben, Le Noble; Wedel: I–II Meyerinck, I–II Itzenplitz; Lattorff: Asseburg, Bornstedt; Goltz: grenadiers Ramin, Wedel; Kalckreuth: grenadiers Manteuffel = 12½ bns.
- First line: left cavalry (G.L. v. Driesen): Meier: Bayreuth Drag. 10; Normann: Driesen Cuir. 5; Krockow (Alt): Carabiniers 5, Krockow Cuir. 5. Infantry (G.L. v. Retzow; Prince Ferdinand of Prussia, Prince Franz of Brunswick): Geist: gren. Schenckendorff, gren. Hacke, Alt-Braunschweig 2, Forcade 2; Münchow: Winterfeldt 2, Geist 2; Kahlden: Pannwitz 2, Kannacher 2, gren. Retzow; Ferdinand/Franz: II–III Garde, Markgraf Karl 2, gren. Kremzow. Right cavalry (G.L. v. Zieten): Schönaich: Baron Schönaich Cuir. 5; Schmettau: Markgraf Friedrich Cuir. 5, Seydlitz Cuir. 5; Lentulus: Gensd'armes 5, Gardes du Corps 3. Flanks: left (Bornstedt) gren. Dieringshofen, I/Kurssell; right (Rohr) gren. Unruh, gren. Kleist.
- Second line (G.L. v. Forcade): left cavalry (Bredow): Gessler Cuir. 5, Prinz Schönaich Cuir. 5, Kyau Cuir. 5. Infantry: Oldenburg: I–II Kalckstein, Prinz Heinrich, gren. Kahlden, Prinz Ferdinand, gren. Ploetz; Bülow: gren. Ostenreich, I/Alt-Württemberg, Jung-Braunschweig, Münchow, Prinz von Preußen. Right cavalry: Krockow (Jung): Stechow Drag. 5, Krockow Drag. 5; Czettritz: Czettritz Drag. 5, Normann Drag. 5.
- Strength: avant-garde 12½ bns 50 sqns; first line 20 bns 48 sqns; flanks 4 bns; second line 11 bns 35 sqns; total 47½ bns (gren. Burgsdorff left at Neumarkt), 133 sqns; 78 heavy guns (10 Brummer, 39 12-pdr field guns, 14 [24-pdr?], 8 howitzers, 7 mortars) and 94 battalion guns.

- Avant-gardes: Luszinsky (hussars, Croats); Nostitz and Morocz (Prinz Karl, Brühl and Prinz Albrecht chevau-legers (Saxon), Croats, Nádasdy and Dessewffy hussars): 12 sqns, 3 Croat bns, 4 hussar regts.
- First line (left to right, i.e. south to north): left cavalry (Serbelloni): Hessen-Darmstadt Drag. 6, Erzherzog Ferdinand Cuir. 5, Kalkreuth Cuir. 5, Gelhay Cuir. 5, Anspach-Bayreuth Cuir. 5. Infantry (FZM Colloredo, FZM Kheul): Erzherzog Karl 2, Hildburghausen 2, Moltke 1, Kheul 2, Thürheim 1, Nic. Esterházy 2, Jos. Esterházy 2, Alt-Wolfenbüttel 2, Arenberg 1, Puebla 2, Leopold Daun 2, Botta 2, Neipperg 2, Kaiser 2. Right cavalry (Lucchesi): Löwenstein Cuir. 5, Stampach Cuir. 5, Lucchesi Cuir. 5, Erzherzog Leopold Cuir. 5, Erzherzog Joseph Drag. 5. = 25 bns 51 sqns.
- Second line: left cavalry (Stampach): Kolowrat Drag. 6, Birkenfeld Cuir. 5, Schmerzing Cuir. 5, O'Donell Cuir. 5. Infantry (Wied, Haller, Winulf Starhemberg): Harrach 2, Ludwig Wolfenbüttel 2, Gaisruck 1, Baden-Baden 1, Harsch 2, Bethlen 1, Browne 1, Roth-Würzburg 1, Deutschmeister 1, Baden-Durlach 1, Kolowrat 2, Pallavicini 1, Wallis 1, Waldeck 1, Karl Lothringen 1. Right cavalry (Esterházy): Anhalt-Zerbst Cuir. 5, Serbelloni Cuir. 5, Württemberg Drag. 5, Benedikt Daun Drag. 5. = 19 bns 41 sqns.
- Reserve corps (FML Duke of Arenberg): Ligne, Sachsen-Gotha, Königsegg, Los Rios, Mercy, Andlau, Haller, d'Arberg = 8 bns.
- Nádasdy's corps: cavalry (O'Donell): Sachsen-Gotha Drag. 5, Jung-Modena Drag. 5, Zweibrücken Drag. 5, Batthyányi Drag. 6; Württemberg contingent (Spiznass): grenadier bns I–III, Spiznass 2, Leib-Regt 2, Prinz Louis 2, Roeder 2, Truchsess 2; Austrian (Forgách): Johann Pálffy, Adam Batthyányi, Forgách, Clerici, Luzan, Maguire, Haller, Leopold Pálffy, Heinrich Daun 2; Bavarian contingent (Seyssel d'Aix): Minucci 2, Morawitzky 2, Leib-Regt 2, Herzog Clemens 2, Kurprinz 2 (and Preysing). = 33 bns 21 sqns.
- Total 85 bns, 125 sqns, 3 Croat bns, 4 hussar regts, about 170 battalion guns and 65 heavy guns.

## Narratives
- `carlyle_leuthen.txt`: Thomas Carlyle, *History of Friedrich II of Prussia*, Book XVIII ch. X (1865), from Tempelhof (eyewitness), Archenholtz, Retzow, Kutzen 1851, Ligne's journal, Cogniazzo. Nostitz's outpost of 3 Saxon dragoon (chevau-leger) regiments and 2 hussar regiments taken at Borne ("540 prisoners"); Frederick on the Scheuberg (Schönberg) for some three hours; the four columns become two and turn south towards the Zobtenberg; the hymn heard on the march; Lucchesi's three messages; Daun takes the reserve horse and much of the left-wing horse north; Frederick on the Lobetinz windmill; Nádasdy's flank en potence at Sagschütz with a 14-gun battery; Ligne's battalion, the orchard ditches west of Leuthen and the windmill. Represents the older Gaudi–Tempelhof tradition.
- `wp_en.txt` (Wikipedia, citing Duffy 1985/2003, Showalter, Redman, Asprey, Blanning, Bodart, Clodfelter): 66,000 Austrians, 33,000–36,000 Prussians, ~167 guns; losses Austrian 22,000 (3,000 dead, 7,000 wounded, 12,000 captured), 51 standards and 116 of 250 guns; Prussian 6,344 (1,141 dead, 5,118 wounded, 85 captured). Contains errors (Lucchesi "decapitated", 04:00 start in four columns towards the Austrian right). `wp_de.txt`, `wp_pl.txt` (Polish modern names).
