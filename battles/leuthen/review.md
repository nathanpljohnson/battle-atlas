## Verdict

Publishable after the small corrections below. The reconstruction has a defensible archival cartographic foundation, distinguishes the Austrian camp from its battle position, and discloses interpolation and important disputes. Its broad tactical story agrees with modern military-historical work independent of the author's bibliography. Reliability is high for the sequence and principal locations, medium for detailed movement and timing, and low for individual unit routes after dark. Automated validation (0 errors, 0 warnings) establishes structural consistency, not historical truth. I read brief.md, scenario.py, validation.md and the source notes, inspected check.png and both downloaded order-of-battle plates, and searched independent scholarship. I did not re-georeference all maps or independently establish every regiment's trajectory. The latest scholarly monograph was discoverable but not accessible in full; no claim below pretends otherwise.

## Issues

1. **Medium — brief.md, Overview losses and disagreement 6; scenario.py, meta.forces.end, strengthNote and phase 11.** The stated component arithmetic does not produce 6,380: 63 + 1,116 + 164 + 5,043 = **6,386**. The source notes already identify 6,386, so this is also an internal contradiction. The recorded printed total of 223 officers plus 6,159 men instead produces 6,382. Neither supports an exact 6,380. **Fix:** inspect the original casualty passage and distinguish its printed total from an arithmetic reconstruction. Until settled, use “about 6,400” publicly, retain the competing totals in the brief, and keep any modeled loss distribution explicitly approximate. Do not silently present an editorially corrected total as the source's own number.

2. **Medium — scenario.py, phase 10 teaching text “Shock cavalry.”** “Nothing but more cavalry could have stopped it” is an absolute counterfactual that the cited battle narrative cannot demonstrate. The same scenario describes Bevern's infantry and canister stopping cavalry at phase 5. That does not prove infantry could have stopped this particular charge, but it shows why a universal rule is misleading. **Fix:** describe the documented advantage: Driesen caught committed cavalry in flank, and the defeated horse disrupted its own infantry. Remove the exclusive claim.

3. **Medium — scenario.py, phase 0 and phase 11 confidence; brief.md, phase headings and Confidence.** Both entire phases carry “high” despite the author's explicit low confidence in pre-08:00 columns and post-dark pursuit. A user viewing those phases receives a stronger assurance than the research warrants. The check plot also shows highly specific individual rout destinations beyond Lissa, which are generated with uid-based offsets rather than independently located. **Fix:** set these phases to medium, explain that the narrative events are better established than unit locations, and preserve low confidence on the actual inferred routes. Phase 3's broad concealed march is well supported; its exact intermediate column placements deserve the same distinction.

4. **Low — scenario.py, unit n3 display name; source gs1904_p177.jpg, Nádasdy corps.** “Forgách's Austrians, first line” flattens two command levels. On the downloaded order-of-battle plate Forgách commands the Austrian infantry grouping containing both subdivisions; the subdivision with Maguire, Haller, Leopold Pálffy and Heinrich Daun is headed **von Arenberg**, while Wolffersdorff heads the other. The displayed n3 regiments themselves match that subdivision. **Fix:** label n3 “Arenberg's Austrians under Forgách” (or otherwise make the hierarchy explicit), without confusing this officer with the Duke of Arenberg commanding the separate Reserve corps. No positional change follows from this naming correction.

5. **Low — brief.md, Sources / modern scholarship; scenario.py, source catalogue.** Post-1990 scholarship currently reaches the reconstruction largely through Wikipedia, although freely accessible modern military-historical analysis can now be added directly. T. G. Otte's *Leuthen: Great Battles* (Oxford University Press, 2024) is also absent. Its publisher abstract treats Austrian intelligence and decisions alongside Prussian tactics and discusses the battle's later national mythology. **Fix:** add the accessible ZMSBw analysis below as a direct cross-check; list Otte honestly as identified but not read unless the text becomes available. Keep the documented 1904 reading where modern summaries differ, and acknowledge those differences rather than replacing it automatically.

## Verified

The following six specific checks use Hans-Peter Kriemann's modern institutional account, which the author did not list. These checks establish agreement with an independent publication, not independent archival proof for every detail:

1. **Army sizes:** its companion ZMSBw podcast description gives 35,000 Prussians and 65,000 Austrians, matching the reconstruction. [ZMSBw, episode 49](https://zms.bundeswehr.de/de/mediathek/zmsbw-podcast-49-leuthen-5532442).
2. **Battle geography:** the article places the Austrian main battle line immediately west of Leuthen, supporting the chosen side of the villages.
3. **Concealed march:** it identifies the Sophienberg as cover for the southward Prussian movement.
4. **Attack timing:** it dates the main attack to 13:00, agreeing with phase 5.
5. **Prussian right:** it places Zieten's cavalry and Bevern's six infantry battalions together on the right flank.
6. **Final cavalry action:** it gives Lucchesi approximately 70 squadrons and Driesen 50, with Württemberg supporting the Prussian flank attack.

Items 2–6: [Kriemann, “Die Schlacht von Leuthen,” ZMSBw, 2 December 2022](https://zms.bundeswehr.de/de/mediathek/die-schlacht-von-leuthen-1757-5535676).

**Disagreements retained:** Kriemann attributes Borne to Zieten and places the opening Brummer fire on the Butterberg; the reconstruction instead follows the more explicit 1904 investigation and distinguishes the later Glanzberg position. These are grounds to cite a disagreement, not sufficient evidence by themselves to overturn the reconstruction. His final phase begins around 16:00, which supports the general late-afternoon sequence but does not validate an exact 16:30 charge. Keeping the latter as an inference is appropriate.

**Order-of-battle check against supplied primary framework:** the Prussian plate explicitly prints 47½ battalions, 133 squadrons, 78 heavy guns and 94 battalion guns. It also distinguishes 4 Seydlitz and 6 Warnery hussar squadrons, so the scenario's 30-squadron reserve grouping is internally defensible. The Austrian plate prints 85 battalions and 125 squadrons plus light troops, and its annotation explains that the order is a reconstruction because no battle order had been found in Vienna. These are checks of the author's supplied evidence, not additional independent sources.
