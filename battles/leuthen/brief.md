# Leuthen, 5 December 1757: research brief

Frederick II's Prussian army attacked the Austrian army of Prince Charles of Lorraine and Field Marshal Daun between Nippern and Sagschütz, 15–20 km west of Breslau (now Lutynia and its neighbours, Lower Silesia, Poland). After a cavalry fight at Borne and a show of deployment towards the Austrian right, Frederick marched south behind a chain of low rises, turned the Austrian left at Sagschütz with an echeloned attack (the "oblique order"), forced the Austrians to wheel and fight round Leuthen, and broke them at dusk with Driesen's cavalry. It is the companion of Rossbach (5 November), a month earlier.

Weather: snow on 3–4 December; a clear, mild night; frozen ground with thin snow; **dense fog until towards midday**, then clear; snow again after dark [gs1904]. Sunrise about 07:55, sunset about 16:00 (local solar time; latitude 51.1°, solar declination −22.4°); civil dusk ended about 16:40.

## Phases

Times are local solar time. Positions are read from the georeferenced Plans 12A/12B unless stated; "Plan 12A C" means the position lettered C on that plan.

### 0. 07:30 — Night camps, march from Neumarkt (conf: high)
- Austrians in their night camp **east** of Nippern, Frobelwitz and Leuthen in two lines, about 7 km long, facing west; Nádasdy's corps and the Reserve corps as a third line behind (east); baggage behind the Weistritz [gs1904] [gs12a A] [beaulieu A]. Leuthen, Frobelwitz and Nippern held by grenadier companies, pickets and guns [gs1904] [beaulieu G].
- Nostitz's three Saxon chevau-leger regiments and Morocz's hussars as an outpost west of Borne; Croats in the Borne and Lampersdorf woods [gs1904].
- Prussians on the march from Neumarkt since about 05:00 in four columns (infantry inside, cavalry outside), the avant-garde (all hussars, the free battalions and Jäger, the Württemberg Dragoons, 9 battalions) ahead; joined between Neumarkt and Kammendorf at 06:00 [gs1904]. Dense fog [gs1904].

### 1. 08:30 — Borne (conf: high)
- 30 squadrons of the Prussian first line charge Nostitz's line west of Borne in the fog; Szekely Hussars take its right flank; the Saxons and Austrian hussars flee through Borne; about 600 prisoners, 11 officers and 2 standards; pursuit past Gross-Heidau to the Austrian front [gs1904] [carlyle (540 prisoners)].
- Contact was not before 08:00, full daylight [gs1904 Anhang 8].
- On the news, Charles moves the whole army 1,000–2,000 paces **forward to the battle position west of the villages**: infantry in two lines on the crest from SE of Nippern past the west side of Frobelwitz to Leuthen, bending round Leuthen; Lucchesi's right-wing cavalry against a wood SE of Nippern; four heavy batteries before the front [gs1904] [gs12a C] [beaulieu F]. Nádasdy to the Sagschütz heights with a flank facing SW, two batteries and an abatis before the Kiefernberg; Nostitz joins him [gs1904].
- Positions: Austrian first line about 600–800 m west of the night line, facing west-south-west; Nádasdy's line running from 1 km north of Sagschütz through the village and 1 km south-east to the Faulbusch, facing west then south-west [gs12a C1, B].

### 2. 10:30 — The feint towards Frobelwitz (conf: medium)
- Frederick on the Schönberg, 1.5 km NE of Borne, sees the Austrian line from end to end, all but the right-wing cavalry behind the Zettelbusch; he decides to attack Nádasdy [gs1904] [carlyle].
- The avant-garde cavalry forms about 1,000 paces east of Borne; the columns pass north and south of Borne and begin to deploy as if against the Austrian right north of Frobelwitz [gs1904].
- Charles and Daun, on the windmill height of the Breslauer Berg (1 km E of Frobelwitz), believe the attack is coming against the right; Lucchesi begs for help; Charles sends the Reserve corps (3 bns into Nippern, 5 SE of it) and Serbelloni with most of the left-wing cavalry north towards Guckerwitz, Daun with them [gs1904] [carlyle] [gs12a C2].
- Debate: whether the deployment east of Borne was a deliberate feint (Lentulus, Barsewisch, Tempelhof; Austrian reports unanimous that they saw it) or, as Gaudi thought, simply the columns following the avant-garde before orders were given [gs1904 Anhang 12]. The map follows gs1904: a deliberate, partial deployment.

### 3. 11:30 — The march south behind the rises (conf: high)
- The army changes from wing-wise to line-wise columns and turns south, direction point the Zobtenberg, passing west of the Schleier-, Butter-, Sophien-, Heide- and Wachberg, about 1.5–2.5 km west of the Austrian front [gs1904] [carlyle].
- 25 hussar squadrons screen the march on the ridge to the east, with the king and Moritz; the heavy guns march left of the first line; ammunition wagons between the lines [gs1904].
- From the Frobelwitz heights the Austrians could see the turn south, not the march: in 1904 a white flag carried along the route was never visible from the Breslauer Berg [gs1904 Anhang 14]. Daun: "the good folk are slipping off; let them go" [gs1904] [carlyle].
- Driesen's left-wing cavalry, the Puttkamer Hussars with it, stays behind the Sophienberg facing east to cover the left [gs1904].

### 4. 12:30 — Deployment at Schriegwitz (conf: high)
- Soon after 12:00 the column heads reach Schriegwitz; the line forms by wheeling platoons left, facing NE between Lobetinz and Schriegwitz [gs1904] [gs12a E].
- Zieten's 53 squadrons through and round Schriegwitz, south and east of it; Bevern's 6 battalions as their flank guard; Wedel's 3 battalions (the "Attacke") ahead of the infantry's right, the Brummer battery to their left; Driesen north of the Heideberg facing east [gs1904].
- The king on the Wachberg south of Lobetinz [gs1904].

### 5. 13:00 — Wedel takes the Kiefernberg (conf: high)
- Wedel advances half-right on the wooded Kiefernberg south of Sagschütz; the Brummer battery fires from the Glanzberg; a wet, willow-lined ditch 300 paces short of the hill; after one volley Wedel goes in with the bayonet; three Württemberg battalions on the hill give way, and the Württembergers to their left and three grenadier battalions in the Faulbusch fall back [gs1904] [beaulieu G]. Seven regimental guns taken [gs1904].
- Nádasdy's 43 squadrons charge Zieten from behind the Faulbusch; Bevern's battalions stop them with musketry and canister; Nostitz falls with fourteen wounds [gs1904].
- The first line follows Wedel in battalion echelons 50 paces apart, 100 paces deep: the oblique order [gs1904] [carlyle].

### 6. 14:00 — Nádasdy's corps rolled up (conf: medium)
- North of Sagschütz the Bavarians and Austrian regiments pushed in front of them give way (Maguire, Johann Pálffy, Haller) [gs1904]; the echelons, with batteries on the Judenberg, roll up the corps from its left [gs1904].
- Wedel and grenadier battalion Kremzow (led by Moritz) take the Austrian battery on the Kirchberg, 600 m NE of Sagschütz [gs1904]. Nádasdy rallies behind the Gohlau ditch, and is outflanked again by Bevern's battalions [gs1904].
- Zieten leads his 53 squadrons between the Faulbusch and the Gohlau ponds; piecemeal charges over ditches; Lentulus takes 15 guns and nearly destroys the Jung-Modena Dragoons; Seelen's Zieten Hussars take about 2,000 prisoners [gs1904] [beaulieu R, S].
- Nádasdy's cavalry rallies north of the Rathen wood; his infantry flees to it and behind the Weistritz [gs1904].

### 7. 15:00 — The Austrian wheel to Leuthen (conf: medium)
- Charles orders a general left wheel with Leuthen as the pivot and sends the Reserve corps back south through Leuthen; Arenberg tries to form a front south of the village with a refused flank; Serbelloni's remaining squadrons cover it until driven behind the Leuthen wood [gs1904] [gs12b B].
- The Austrian battalions south of Leuthen give way to the heights north of the village, where the infantry is crammed "in places 100 ranks deep" [gs1904] [gs12b C]. All remaining artillery on the windmill height north of Leuthen [gs1904].
- The Prussian line advances frontally on Leuthen, from the Butterberg to the Leuthen wood [gs12b A]. The king near Radaxdorf [gs1904].

### 8. 15:30 — Storm of Leuthen (conf: high)
- The infantry storms the 1,800-pace front of the village [gs1904]. Roth-Würzburg holds the walled churchyard; the Garde and grenadier battalion Retzow assault in vain until III/Garde under Captain von Möllendorff forces the west gate; a breach is shot in the south wall; only 4 officers and 33 men of Roth-Würzburg escape [gs1904] [wiki].
- The village is cleared after about half an hour of house-to-house fighting [gs1904] [carlyle]. Ligne's battalion (Ligne regiment of the Reserve) loses nearly all its officers west of the village [carlyle] [wiki].
- The Prussian left comes up level west of Leuthen into a firefight with the refused Austrian right; the king brings the left-wing heavy battery and the Brummers to the SE slope of the Butterberg [gs1904].

### 9. 16:15 — Stalemate north of Leuthen; Lucchesi moves (conf: medium)
- The Prussian line pushes north of Leuthen against the Austrian mass along the deep ditch at the north edge of the village; "the battle stood; the short day was ending" [gs1904].
- More than 70 squadrons under Lucchesi and Serbelloni have gathered SE of Gross-Heidau; Lucchesi sets off to strike between the Butterberg and Leuthen at the apparently open Prussian left, without scouting south [gs1904] [gs12b D].
- Time: not stated in gs1904; inferred from the half-hour fight that began about 15:30 and the coming of darkness (sunset about 16:00).

### 10. 16:30 — Driesen's charge (conf: medium)
- Driesen, watching from the Sophienberg, moves north behind the rises, wheels right and takes Lucchesi in the flank: 15 cuirassier squadrons and the 10 squadrons of the Bayreuth Dragoons in the first line, 15 cuirassier squadrons in the second, the Puttkamer Hussars overlapping on the left and into the rear; Prince Eugen of Württemberg's 30 squadrons join [gs1904] [gs12b C]. Lucchesi is killed; his horse flees into its own infantry [gs1904] [wiki].
- Panic in the Austrian right; the whole army makes for the Weistritz; Wallis and Baden-Durlach stand on the windmill height and are ridden down; grenadier battalion Schenckendorff takes the batteries there; Angelelli's free battalions and Jäger come up from Borne [gs1904].

### 11. 18:00 — Night: Lissa (conf: high)
- The flight pours over Saara and Klein-Heidau to the Weistritz bridges; Nádasdy holds the crossings at Lissa; the Prussian infantry halts north of the Breslau road [gs1904].
- About 18:00, in snow and darkness, the king with grenadier battalions Manteuffel, Wedel and Ramin and the Seydlitz Cuirassiers goes forward to Lissa; a last fight for the bridges east of the town shortly before 19:00; the king spends the night in the Lissa Schloss [gs1904].
- The Prussian army camps between Lissa and Guckerwitz [gs1904].

## Disagreements and choices
1. **Where the Austrians stood.** The 19th-century Prussian plans, all derived from Gaudi (incl. gs1824), and the modern USMA-style maps put the Austrian line along the night camp east of Frobelwitz and Leuthen. gs1904 (Anhang 10), using the Walter–Wiercker plan in the Vienna Kriegsarchiv, Gadow's plan, Charles's own relation ("une heure devant le jour … on prit une autre position") and Beaulieu's legend (F: "how far the army advanced to occupy the heights"), puts the battle position 1,000–2,000 paces west of the villages on the crest. **Chosen: gs1904** (archival, and independently supported by the Austrian staff's own published plan).
2. **Strengths.** Austrian 60,000–90,000 in contemporary estimates; 65,000 in gs1904's calculation from the Breslau strength return less detachments and losses; 66,000 in Wikipedia. Prussian 33,000–39,000. **Chosen: gs1904's 35,000 and 65,000.**
3. **The feint** (phase 2): deliberate (most sources) or accidental (Gaudi). **Chosen: deliberate, partial** [gs1904].
4. **Nostitz's wound.** Carlyle has him mortally wounded at Borne in the morning; gs1904 (Anhang 9, from the Saxon officer Rex's report and Gadow) has him wounded and captured in the afternoon charge on Zieten. **Chosen: afternoon.**
5. **Who led the Borne charge.** Zieten in some accounts; gs1904 finds no evidence and gives it to the avant-garde cavalry under Prince Eugen of Württemberg. **Chosen: Württemberg.**
6. **Prussian losses** 6,344 (Wikipedia) vs 6,380 (gs1904's components; its printed total, 223 officers and 6,159 men, does not add up). **Chosen: 6,380.** Austrian 22,000; trophies 116 or 131 guns. **Chosen: gs1904** for both.
7. **The time of Lucchesi's charge** is not given in gs1904; 16:30 is inferred (phase 9).
8. **The "oblique order".** gs1904 calls Leuthen "the first and only time" it was carried right through; it also records that the flanking advance turned frontal south of Leuthen. Modern writers (via Wikipedia) stress the terrain and the screened march more than a drill formula. The map shows the echelon from 13:00 and a frontal fight from 15:00.

## Myths
- **"Nun danket alle Gott" sung by the whole army** after the battle: gs1904 presents it as fact (individual men during the night march to Lissa, then regiment after regiment); Kroener (2000) analyses its growth into a 19th-century national myth, and Wikipedia calls the story "likely apocryphal". The map mentions it only as a tradition.
- **"Bon soir, Messieurs" at Lissa**: the story that the king walked into a Schloss full of Austrian officers and bluffed his way out is a legend (Droysen, Kutzen, Grünhagen, Koser; gs1904 Anhang 18); at most he greeted wounded officers there.
- **The Württembergers deserted crying "Long live the King of Prussia"**: not true of the Kiefernberg garrison, which lost 20% before falling back (gs1904 Anhang 15), though the contingent fought reluctantly and lost very many missing.
- **The Parchwitz speech**: its substance is genuine, its familiar wording (Retzow 1802) is a later compilation (gs1904 Anhang 5).
- **Lucchesi decapitated by a cannonball** (Wikipedia): gs1904 says only that he fell in the melee. Not shown.
- **The Austrian line through and east of the villages** (see disagreement 1).

## Confidence
- Phases: high 0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 11; medium 2, 6, 7, 9, 10.
- Units: high for the Austrian battle-position lines, Nádasdy's corps, Wedel, Zieten and the Prussian line at 13:00–15:30 (mapped on Plan 12A/12B); medium for the march columns, the feint, the Reserve corps' movements, Lucchesi and Driesen (mapped on 12B but timed by inference); low for the Prussian columns before 08:00, Nostitz's outpost and the pursuit after dark (narrative only).
- Per-phase strengths are estimates distributed from the sourced totals; unit strengths at phase 0 are estimates from battalion and squadron counts (about 520 men per Prussian battalion and 78 per squadron, 550 per Austrian battalion and 105 per squadron, scaled to the totals).
